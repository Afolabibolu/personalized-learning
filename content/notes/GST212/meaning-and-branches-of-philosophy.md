# Meaning, Methods and Branches of Philosophy

## What philosophy is

Philosophy is not taught in Nigerian primary or secondary schools, so myths surround it: that philosophers are absent-minded or shabby, or that only herbalists or monks can study it. None of these is true. **There is no universally accepted definition of philosophy**, but its **etymology** helps: it comes from the Greek **philos (love)** and **sophia (wisdom)**, so philosophy is the **"love of wisdom"** or pursuit of knowledge, carried out systematically by **reasoning**. It has been defined as a **critical examination of the fundamental issues of our existence** and as the general study of values, morals, logic and the nature of reality.

## Methods and goals

Philosophical methods include:

- **philosophical criticism**: rational appraisal of ideas to arrive at truth;
- **argumentation**: giving arguments for a solution;
- **methodic doubt**: systematically doubting one's beliefs;
- the **dialectical method**: seeking truth through discussion;
- **inquiry**; and
- **analysis**: breaking a complex notion into parts.

Its goals include developing the person to contribute to society, removing **vagueness and ambiguity**, **critical thinking**, spotting **fallacies** and tackling life's problems rationally.

## Two senses

In the **broad or popular sense**, philosophy is **a way of life or worldview** of a people: their fundamental beliefs, as reflected in the Yoruba custom of prostrating to greet elders. In the **technical or academic sense**, it is a discipline of clarifying concepts through reasoning, practised by **professional or analytic philosophers** such as **Bodunrin, Sogolo and Wiredu**. The Ionian philosopher **Thales** held that **water** is the primary substance of all things.

## First- and second-order questions

Philosophy raises **first-order questions** about issues **within philosophy** (in epistemology, ethics and metaphysics) and **second-order questions** about the **foundations of other disciplines**, as in philosophy of law, education or science. It is therefore **foundational** to other fields: **Locke** called philosophy an "under-labourer" and **Kant** the "Queen of the sciences".

## The branches

1. **Epistemology**, the **theory of knowledge** (Greek **episteme**, knowledge, and **logos**, theory), studies the nature, sources, validity and limits of knowledge. **Rationalism** holds that knowledge comes from **reason**; **empiricism**, from **sense experience**. Knowledge is **a priori** (from reason) or **a posteriori** (from experience).
2. **Metaphysics** studies **reality**: what exists, free will, God and evil. It divides into **ontology** (the nature of being) and **cosmology** (the origin of the universe).
3. **Logic** is the study of the general structure of sound reasoning and good arguments.
4. **Ethics (moral philosophy)** is the systematic study of the principles of good behaviour. It divides into **normative ethics** (how people ought to behave), **meta-ethics** (the meaning of moral terms) and **applied ethics** (practical issues such as **abortion and euthanasia**). **Aesthetics**, the study of art and beauty, is a related value theory.
