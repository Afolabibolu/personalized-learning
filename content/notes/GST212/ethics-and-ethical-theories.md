# Ethics and Ethical Theories

## Ethics and the good life

**Ethics** is an **inquiry into the nature of morality**. It clarifies moral concepts and also shows how to live the **good life**, a life of happiness or flourishing. For **Socrates, Plato and Aristotle**, this comes through **virtue**. The classical philosophers named four **cardinal virtues**: **temperance, fortitude, prudence and justice**. The first three build personal character, while **justice aims at other people's well-being**, as do charity, honesty and loyalty. The opposites of virtues are **vices**, such as stealing, lying and cheating; **examination malpractice** is a vice that disrupts the moral order of a school, and the University of Ibadan may expel those involved.

**Ethical theories** are the standards by which moral issues are weighed to decide whether they are right or wrong.

## Deontological theories

**Deontological** theories hold that actions are right or wrong **in themselves** or because they conform to **moral rules and duties**, whatever their consequences. **Act deontology** judges each act by intuition; Kant's categorical imperative is the best-known duty-based theory.

## Consequentialist theories

**Consequentialist (teleological)** theories hold that rightness depends **solely on consequences**. They include:

- **Utilitarianism**: actions are good insofar as they increase happiness and decrease suffering, following the **principle of utility**, "the **greatest happiness for the greatest number**". **Act utilitarianism** judges each action by its consequences; **rule utilitarianism** applies the principle to **rules**.
- **Egoism**: priority to self-interest. **Ethical egoism** says people **ought** to pursue their own interest (normative); **psychological egoism** says they **always do** (descriptive).
- **Altruism**: concern for the interests of others.

## Other theories

- **Virtue ethics** focuses on the **character of the doer** rather than the act; Aristotle's virtues include compassion, honesty, courage, humility and justice.
- **Hedonism** holds that **pleasure is the highest good**. **Ethical hedonism** says we ought to seek pleasure; **psychological hedonism** says we in fact do.
- **Ethical relativism** holds that moral principles are valid only relative to a **place and time**; moral **subjectivism** rests right and wrong on individual feeling, while moral **objectivism** holds there is one correct moral code.
- **Situation ethics** judges actions by their **circumstances**.

## Applied ethics

**Applied ethics** examines practical issues: abortion, euthanasia, suicide, surrogacy, terrorism, the environment, business and professional conduct, and the Internet. A **moral dilemma** is a choice between conflicting moral options. Ethics urges us to follow the options that can best be **defended in the court of rationality**. In traditional African societies, children learnt moral values through **moonlight tales** and family life, and moral decline shows in fraud, embezzlement and cybercrime.
