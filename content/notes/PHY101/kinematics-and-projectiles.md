# Kinematics and Projectiles

## Describing motion

Displacement is position change with direction. **Velocity v = dx/dt** and **acceleration a = dv/dt = d²x/dt²**. The average acceleration over an interval is Δv/Δt. For v = 14 − 8t + t², v(2) = 2 and v(4) = −2, so the average acceleration between them is **−2 m/s²**.

For x = 2t² + 2 and y = t³ − 2, the velocity is (4t, 3t²). At t = 5 that is (20, 75), with magnitude **77.6 m/s**.

## Constant acceleration

- **v = u + at**
- **s = ut + ½at²**
- **v² = u² + 2as**
- **s = ½(u + v)t**

**Examples**:

- A jet landing at 100 m/s on a 1 km runway needs a = −100²/2000 = **−5 m/s²**.
- A snake's head starting at 10 m/s and accelerating at 45 m/s² covers 1 m when 22.5t² + 10t − 1 = 0, so t ≈ **0.08 s**.
- A 1954 rocket sled decelerating from 1020 km/h (283 m/s) in 1.4 s had about 202 m/s². A bullet fired upward has g = 9.8 m/s², so the ratio is **20.7 : 1**.

## Free fall

Near the earth, all bodies fall with **g ≈ 9.8 m/s²** downward when air resistance is neglected. Thrown up at speed u, a body rises to **u²/2g** and returns with the same speed. So two balls thrown up and down from a cliff with equal speeds **land with equal speeds**, whatever their masses.

## Projectiles

The horizontal and vertical motions are **independent**. Horizontally the velocity is constant (aₓ = 0); vertically the acceleration is g downward.

- **Horizontal projection** from height h: t = √(2h/g) and range = uₓt. An aircraft at 40 m/s and 100 m drops relief at t = 4.52 s, so it lands **180.8 m** ahead. It strikes the ground with velocity 40i − 44.3j m/s.
- **Oblique projection** at speed u and angle α:
  - **time of flight T = 2u sin α/g**;
  - **greatest height H = u² sin²α/2g**;
  - **range R = u² sin 2α/g**, the greatest at **45°**.
- At the top of the path the velocity is horizontal, and **perpendicular to the acceleration**.

A cannon ball landing 500 m away when fired at 45° has u = 70 m/s and T = 10.1 s, so after 2 s it is still **moving up**.
