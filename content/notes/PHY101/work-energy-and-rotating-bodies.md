# Work, Energy and Rotating Rigid Bodies

## Work and energy

**Work W = F·d = Fd cos θ**, measured in joules. A force (3, 0, 4) N moving a body through (9, 0, 2) m does 27 + 8 = **35 J**. **Power** is the rate of doing work: P = W/t = F·v, in watts.

- **Kinetic energy** is ½mv².
- **Gravitational potential energy** is mgh.
- **Elastic potential energy** is ½kx².

The **work–energy theorem**: the net work done equals the change in kinetic energy. When only conservative forces act, **KE + PE is constant**. Friction turns mechanical energy into heat: friction work = μR × distance.

**Example**: a ball dropped from 20 m (g = 10) reaches the ground at v = √(2gh) = **20 m/s**, whatever its mass.

## Rotational motion

For rotation about a fixed axis:

- angle θ in radians, angular velocity **ω = dθ/dt** and angular acceleration **α = dω/dt**;
- they relate to linear quantities by **v = ωr** and **a = αr**;
- the constant-α equations mirror the linear ones: ω = ω₀ + αt and **ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ**.

A drum slowing from 600 rpm to 240 rpm over 50 revolutions has α = (8²π² − 20²π²)/(2 × 100π) ≈ **−5.28 rad/s²**.

## Moment of inertia

The **moment of inertia** is **I = Σmᵢrᵢ²**, the rotational counterpart of mass:

- a **uniform rod** about its middle: **ML²/12**; about one end: ML²/3;
- a **disc** about its centre: **½Ma²**;
- a sphere about a diameter: ⅖Ma².

**Parallel axes theorem**: I = I_G + Md². A body's I is the sum of its parts.

## Torque and angular momentum

**Torque τ = Iα** is the rotational form of F = ma. **Angular momentum L = Iω** is a vector along the axis, and it is **conserved** when no external torque acts: a skater who pulls her arms in spins faster. **Rotational kinetic energy** is **½Iω²**. A rolling body has ½mv² + ½Iω².
