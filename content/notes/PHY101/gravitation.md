# Gravitation

## The law of gravitation

**F = Gm₁m₂/r²**, with **G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²**. The force acts along the line joining the masses and is always attractive.

For **three equal masses M** at the corners of an equilateral triangle of side R, each pair attracts with GM²/R². The two forces on each mass are at 60°, so the net force is 2(GM²/R²) cos 30° ≈ **1.7 GM²/R²**.

## g and weight

At a planet's surface, mg = GMm/R², so **g = GM/R²**. Then:

- **weight ∝ M/R²**. If the earth's radius grew tenfold (6400 km to 64,000 km) while its mass doubled, your weight would change by 2/100, a **decrease by a factor of 50**;
- the ratio of weights gives the ratio of g. A man weighing 875 N on earth and 2310 N on Jupiter finds g_J = 9.8 × 2310/875 ≈ **25.9 m/s²**;
- g decreases with height as GM/(R + h)².

## Satellites

A satellite in a circular orbit has gravity supplying its centripetal force: GMm/r² = mv²/r, so

**v = √(GM/r)** and **T = 2π√(r³/GM)**.

Near the surface (r ≈ R), v = √(gR) ≈ 7.9 km/s.

A **parked (geostationary) satellite** has a period equal to the **earth's rotation period about its axis** (24 h), so it stays over one point.

## Kepler's laws and escape

1. Planets move in **ellipses** with the sun at one focus.
2. The line from the sun to a planet sweeps out **equal areas in equal times**.
3. **T² ∝ r³**.

The **escape velocity** from a planet is **√(2GM/R) = √(2gR) ≈ 11.2 km/s** for the earth.

For a short range on earth, treating the trajectory as a projectile, the minimum speed to reach a target at distance d is √(gd). For d = 500 km that is about **2213.6 m/s**.
