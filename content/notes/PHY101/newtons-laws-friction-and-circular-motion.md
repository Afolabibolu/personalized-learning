# Newton's Laws, Friction and Circular Motion

## Newton's laws

1. A body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless a **resultant force** acts (inertia).
2. **F = ma**: the resultant force equals mass × acceleration. More generally, force is the rate of change of momentum.
3. **To every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.**

A body at **constant velocity** has zero resultant force. If one force is 2i − 6j, the other must be **−2i + 6j**.

## Friction and inclines

Friction opposes relative motion. Limiting friction is **F = μR**, where R is the normal reaction. On a plane inclined at θ:

- the weight resolves into **mg sin θ along the plane** and **mg cos θ normal to it**;
- R = mg cos θ;
- sliding down: a = g(sin θ − μ cos θ);
- pulled up by P: a = P/m − g(sin θ + μ cos θ).

If a block slides down a 60° plane with a = g/3, then μ = **(sin θ − ⅓)/cos θ**.

A 10 kg block pulled by 250 N up a 45° plane with μ = 0.5 accelerates at 25 − 1.5 × 9.8 × 0.707 = 14.6 m/s², so it covers **116.8 m** in 4 s.

## Connected bodies

In an **Atwood machine** (masses m₁ > m₂ over a smooth pulley):

- **a = (m₁ − m₂)g/(m₁ + m₂)**;
- **T = 2m₁m₂g/(m₁ + m₂)**.

For 3 kg and 5 kg, T = 30 × 9.8/8 = **36.75 N**.

## Circular motion

A body moving at speed v in a circle of radius r accelerates **towards the centre**: **a = v²/r = ω²r**. It therefore needs a **centripetal force mv²/r**. The moon (r = 3.85 × 10⁸ m, T = 27.3 days) has a = 4π²r/T² ≈ **2.7 × 10⁻³ m/s²** towards the earth.

**Vertical circle**: at the lowest point T = mω²r + mg, the **largest** tension; at the top T = mω²r − mg, the smallest.

A coin on a turntable slips when mω²r exceeds friction. If ω doubles, the slipping radius falls to a quarter: from 4 cm to **1 cm**.

On a flat bend, friction provides the centripetal force, so **v_max = √(μgr)**: with μ = 0.6 and r = 5 m, v ≈ 5.4 m/s.

A **conical pendulum** of length l at angle θ has period 2π√(l cos θ/g).
