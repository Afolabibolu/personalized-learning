# Literature, Its Elements and Figures of Speech

## What literature is

"Literature" comes from the Latin **litera**, to do with writing, but limiting it to written text would exclude **oral** literature. Literature is the **creative or imaginative works (written and spoken) involving human or animal characters which depict human conditions** through language marked by **indirectness** and **literariness**. It is not history: it re-presents society **fictionally**, plausibly but without one-to-one correspondence to reality.

## Relevance

Literature **entertains**, **teaches morals** (it is didactic, and often shows **poetic justice**, the triumph of good over evil, as in Akinjobi's *Mirage*), is a **repository of history** (Achebe's *Things Fall Apart* documents Igbo culture), builds **language proficiency**, is **therapeutic**, contributes to **economies** and performs an **ideological** function.

## Genres

**Genre** (French for kind or type) depends on how a text is composed. The **three main genres** are:

- **Drama**: impersonation, action, dialogue, stage, audience and costume, realised through **performance**; types include tragedy, comedy and tragicomedy.
- **Poetry**: written in **lines, verses and stanzas**, marked by **rhythm, imagery and language**; types include ballad, lyric, sonnet, elegy and ode.
- **Prose**: tells a story in episodes or chapters, as fiction or non-fiction.

## Elements

- **Subject matter**: the topic the author writes about.
- **Theme**: the **central idea or underlying meaning**; subject matter is **broader** than theme.
- **Point of view**: first-person or third-person (omniscient or limited).
- **Plot**: the sequence of events with emphasis on **cause and effect**, in five parts: **exposition, complication, climax, falling action and denouement**.
- **Setting**: the **time and place**, temporal and spatial; *Macbeth* is set in medieval Scotland.
- **Character and characterisation**: characters perform the actions; characterisation is how writers present them, by **telling** and **showing**.

## Figures of speech

- **Imagery** paints mental pictures: visual, auditory, gustatory, olfactory, tactile and kinaesthetic.
- **Simile** compares with **like** or **as**: "as gentle as a dove".
- **Metaphor** compares **without** like or as: "Satan is a roaring lion".
- **Personification** gives human qualities to non-humans: "The sky is weeping".
- **Irony** says the opposite of what is meant.
- **Hyperbole** exaggerates.
- **Oxymoron** places contradictory words side by side ("truthful lies").
- **Paradox** seems absurd but holds a truth.
- **Antithesis** balances opposites ("Man proposes, God disposes").
- **Euphemism** states something unpleasant mildly ("joined his ancestors").
- **Metonymy** uses an associated object ("The pen is mightier than the sword").
- **Synecdoche** uses a part for the whole ("more hands").
- **Alliteration** repeats initial consonants, **assonance** repeats vowels, **anaphora** repeats words at the start of lines, **onomatopoeia** imitates sound, and **apostrophe** addresses the absent.
