# Drama and Poetry Appreciation

## Drama

**Drama** is the genre that involves **impersonation, action, dialogue, stage, audience, costume, mime and movement**, and it is **realised through performance**. A play is divided into **acts and scenes**, its story is told through the **dialogue** of characters, and the playwright's **stage directions** describe setting and action.

- **Tragedy** shows the downfall of a central character, ending in suffering or death and arousing pity and fear.
- **Comedy** is light, often satirical, and ends happily.
- **Tragicomedy** mixes the two.

Plays often rest on **poetic justice**, where good is rewarded and evil punished. To appreciate a play, identify the **speaker** and the **addressee** of an extract, the **subject of discussion**, the **setting**, the **conflict**, and the roles of the **protagonist** and **antagonist**.

The set play is **J. M. Synge's *Riders to the Sea***, a one-act tragedy set on an island off the west coast of Ireland. Maurya, an old woman who has already lost her husband and several sons to the sea, loses her last son, Bartley, as he rides to the horse fair. It explores **human helplessness before nature**, grief and acceptance.

## Poetry

**Poetry** is written in **lines, verses and stanzas**, and its distinctive features are **rhythm, imagery and its language**. The person speaking is the **poetic persona**. Types include the **ballad** (a narrative song), **lyric** (personal feeling), **sonnet** (fourteen lines), **elegy** (a lament for the dead), **ode** (praise of a person or thing) and **epic**.

## Appreciating a poem

To appreciate a poem, read it several times and ask:

- **Subject matter and theme**: what is it about, and what does it say?
- **Mood and tone**: what is the persona's mood (sad, angry, joyful)?
- **Imagery**: which senses are appealed to (visual, auditory, tactile and so on)?
- **Figures of speech**: simile, metaphor, personification, apostrophe, repetition and others.
- **Sound devices**: rhythm, rhyme, **alliteration** and **assonance**.
- **Structure**: lines, stanzas and any refrain.

## The selected poems (2025/2026)

The reading list names eight poems: "When a Strainer Takes in Water" (translated by Olabimtan), David Diop's "Africa", Letlhogonolo Swarathe's "I am a Feminist, Not Mere Ash", William Wordsworth's "My Heart Leaps Up", W. B. Yeats's "The Second Coming", R. E. G. Armattoe's "Servant-kings", Tayo Olafioye's "The Conclave of Hyenas" and Claude McKay's "If We Must Die". For example, "If We Must Die" urges the oppressed to resist and die nobly rather than like hogs, while "My Heart Leaps Up" celebrates joy in nature: "The Child is father of the Man".
