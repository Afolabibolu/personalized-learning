# Punctuation and Forms of Writing

## Punctuation

**Punctuation marks** divide written language into **readable bits** to aid comprehension.

- **Full stop (.)**: ends a sentence, marks **abbreviations** (Ph.D.) and, as an ellipsis, shows **omission**.
- **Question mark (?)**: ends a **direct question**.
- **Exclamation mark (!)**: shows exclamation or strong emotion ("Eureka!").
- **Capital letters** begin sentences and **proper nouns** (Easter, April).
- **Comma (,)**: shows **separation and a brief pause**, as in lists and appositives ("Titi, the new girl, has secured a room").
- **Apostrophe (')**: shows **possession** ("Miss Sango's fiancé"; "the rooms' décor"), **omission** in contractions (there's) and some plurals.
- **Colon (:)**: connects an **additional piece of information**, such as a list.
- **Semicolon (;)**: joins **related independent clauses** instead of a conjunction ("The rains are here; my farmer-dad is happy").
- **Quotation marks**: single or double, show words quoted **exactly** as said or written.
- **Brackets**: enclose extra information the sentence can do without.
- **Hyphen (-)**: joins two words into one ("a two-day seminar").
- **Dash**: shows an interruption ("many gifts – too many to count").
- **Slash (/)**: separates **alternatives** ("and/or"; 2019/2020).
- **Italics, bold and underlining** foreground titles and foreign words.

## Official correspondence

- A **formal letter** has the writer's **address** and **date**, the recipient's address, a **salutation** ("Dear Sir"), a **title**, a body in clear paragraphs, and a **complimentary close** ("Yours faithfully") with signature and full name.
- A **memorandum (memo)** is an internal message within an organisation, headed **To, From, Date and Subject**, with no salutation.
- **Minutes** are the official record of a meeting: those present and absent, apologies, the agenda items, decisions taken, and when the meeting ended, signed by the secretary and chairperson.
- A **report** presents findings on an issue: title, terms of reference, **introduction**, **findings**, **conclusions** and **recommendations**.

## Essays, term papers and book reviews

An **essay** has an **introduction**, a **body** of well-developed paragraphs, each with a **topic sentence**, and a **conclusion**. Its types include **narrative**, **descriptive**, **expository** and **argumentative**. A **term paper** is a researched academic paper with a clear **thesis**, evidence from **sources**, proper **citations** and a **reference list**. A **book review** gives the book's details, a summary, an evaluation of its strengths and weaknesses, and a recommendation.
