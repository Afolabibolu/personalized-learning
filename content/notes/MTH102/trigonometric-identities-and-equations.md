# Trigonometric Identities and Equations

## Basic identities

From the unit circle, **sin²θ + cos²θ = 1**. Dividing by cos²θ gives **1 + tan²θ = sec²θ**, and dividing by sin²θ gives **1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ**.

## Addition formulae

- **sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B**
- **cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B**
- **tan(A ± B) = (tan A ± tan B)/(1 ∓ tan A tan B)**

Setting B = A gives the **double angles**:

- **sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A**
- **cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A = 1 − 2sin²A = 2cos²A − 1**

So **(1 − cos 2θ)/sin 2θ = tan θ**, and the exact value **sin 15° = sin(45° − 30°) = (√6 − √2)/4**. The co-function rule gives cos(90° − (A + B)) = sin(A + B).

**Products to sums** (from adding the addition formulae):

- cos x cos z = ½[cos(x + z) + cos(x − z)]
- sin x sin z = ½[cos(x − z) − cos(x + z)]
- sin x cos z = ½[sin(x + z) + sin(x − z)]

With x + z = U and x − z = V, they become **sums to products**: cos U + cos V = 2 cos((U + V)/2) cos((U − V)/2), and similarly for the others.

## The R sin(x + α) form

a sin x + b cos x = **R sin(x + α)** with **R = √(a² + b²)** and **tan α = b/a**. For example, 24 sin x + 7 cos x = 25 sin(x + α) with tan α = 7/24, so α ≈ 16.26°. To solve 24 sin x + 7 cos x = 5, solve sin(x + α) = 1/5.

## Solving equations

Reduce the equation to one function, factorise, and give every solution in the range (or the **general solution**).

- **cos 2θ + sin θ = 0** becomes 1 − 2sin²θ + sin θ = 0, so (2 sin θ + 1)(sin θ − 1) = 0. Then sin θ = 1, giving θ = π/2 + 2nπ, or sin θ = −½, giving θ = nπ + (−1)ⁿ⁺¹π/6.
- **sin 2x = tan x** becomes 2 sin x cos x = sin x/cos x, so sin x = 0 or cos²x = ½.

In the fourth quadrant, cos is positive and sin negative: if cot²θ = 4 there, then tan θ = −½.

**Inverse functions**: sin⁻¹ x is the angle in [−π/2, π/2] whose sine is x, so sin⁻¹(½) = π/6.
