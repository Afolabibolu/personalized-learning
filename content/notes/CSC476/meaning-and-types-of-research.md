# The Meaning and Types of Research

## What research is

**Research** (re + search, "to search again") is a systematic investigation to gain new knowledge. It brings new knowledge to light, corrects mistakes and removes misconceptions. It is the systematic collection, analysis and interpretation of data to answer a question or solve a problem. The Higher Education Funding Council for England defines it as original investigation undertaken to gain knowledge and understanding.

**The knowledge hierarchy:** **data** are facts accepted at face value, such as measurements; data interpreted becomes **information**; **knowledge** is a higher-level understanding of the "why", or information combined with experience, context and reflection (Davenport et al., 1998). Scientific knowledge is organized into **theories**, which are consistent with verified theory, supported by many strands of evidence, make testable predictions, survive tests that could prove them false, and are the best explanation by Occam's razor.

## Originality

Research must contribute something new to the world's knowledge. Cryer (1996) lists areas of originality: **exploring the unknown**, **exploring the unanticipated**, **new use of data**, and **new tools, techniques, procedures and methods**. Wrong reasons for doing research include "to have time for other things", "because I have to", "it looks good in my CV" and "I have all the data ready".

## Characteristics of research

Research demands a **clear statement of the problem**, requires a **plan**, **builds on existing data** (positive and negative findings), and collects **new data** organized to answer the research questions.

## The dialectic of research

Contributions to a field's debate are a **thesis** (an original idea), an **antithesis** (an argument challenging a thesis) or a **synthesis** (a new argument from existing sources that resolves a contradiction), as in the debate over prototyping.

## Types of research

- **Basic (pure):** explores a concept without regard to a specific problem. **Applied:** solves a specific problem or practical question.
- **Quantitative:** measurement on numeric scales, from the natural sciences; answers how much, how many, how often; accuracy and repeatability matter. **Qualitative:** from the social sciences; answers why, how, in what way; uses case studies and surveys; repeatability is a problem.
- **Primary:** collects original data, such as by questionnaire. **Secondary:** uses existing data, such as census records.

## Computer science research

Computer science research includes **pure theory**, **descriptive**, **exploratory** (no earlier model), **explanatory** (why something happens), **causal**, **normative** (rules, standards, algorithms), **problem-solving**, **development and application** studies, **action research**, **case studies**, **surveys**, **experimental or empirical research**, **hypothesis-based studies** and **computer simulation**. Experiments test theories and support induction, deriving theories from observation; the Knight and Leveson experiment falsified the assumption that faults in different versions of a multiversion program are independent.
