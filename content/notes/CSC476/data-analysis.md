# Data Analysis

## The analysis plan

To analyse data systematically, write an **analysis plan** at the start of the project, describing the research question and capturing the hypotheses. It may be amended as the research progresses.

## Measurement scales

The first consideration is the measurement scale of each variable:

- **Nominal:** categories only, with no order, such as gender coded 1 and 2.
- **Ordinal:** categories in rank order, but with unequal or unknown gaps, such as primary, secondary and tertiary health facilities.
- **Continuous:** rank order with equal units of measurement.
- **Ratio:** like continuous, but with an **absolute zero**, such as Body Mass Index.

Continuous and ratio data are **parametric**: they can be added, subtracted, multiplied and divided, with the mean as the measure of central tendency and the variance as the measure of dispersion. Nominal and ordinal data are **non-parametric** and are analysed with non-parametric tests.

## Descriptive statistics

Descriptive statistics show how data vary or relate. A **frequency distribution** groups values into intervals: many intervals limit the loss of information, while few make the distribution easier to read, and unequal intervals should be used with caution.

- **Central tendency:** the **mean** is simple to calculate and to combine across sub-populations, but is often used inappropriately because extreme values affect it. The **median**, the middle value, is unchanged when the largest value increases or the smallest decreases. In a normal (unskewed) distribution, the mean and median are the same.
- **Dispersion:** how spread out the values are, such as the range, variance and standard deviation.
- **Sub-group analysis** compares groups within the data.

## Statistical tests

- **Association or correlation:** the variables are equivalent, with no independent or dependent variable. Use the **chi-square test** for nominal or ordinal data and **Pearson correlation** for continuous or ratio data.
- **Causality by group comparison:** for example the independent chi-square test for nominal or ordinal outcomes.
- **Causality by prediction:** **regression analysis** predicts an outcome from several variables: **linear regression** for a normally distributed continuous outcome, and **logistic regression** for a dichotomous outcome such as yes or no.

## Qualitative data analysis

Qualitative analysis consists of **data management**, **data reduction** and **coding**, often helped by software such as Atlas.ti. Good practice includes systematic data collection, carefully specified analysis with attention to **validity** and **reliability**, and **triangulation** with other methods.

The research team must ensure **rigour**, for example through member checks, more than one coder, and triangulation. In quantitative studies, **reliability** means repeatability and independence of the findings from the researcher; in qualitative research, it means that, given the data collected, the results are consistent. The strength of qualitative research lies in **validity**, closeness to the truth: in-depth understanding helps counter alternative explanations.
