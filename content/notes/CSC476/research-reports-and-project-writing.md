# Research Reports and Computer Science Project Writing

## The research report format

A research report has:

1. **Preliminary section:** title page, preface, table of contents, lists of tables and of figures.
2. **Introduction:** background; the problem; its relation to previous theory and research; aim and objectives; importance; hypotheses (if needed); delimitations; assumptions; definitions of important terms.
3. **Literature review:** the theoretical framework, with the basic concepts and theory of the domain, and the most relevant, current related works.
4. **Methods:** for empirical software engineering and social computing research, how the study was conducted, the population and sample, the subjects and how they were assigned and measured, the instruments and their reliability and validity. For other computing research, the framework or model and how it works, its assumptions, the algorithm, the data (sources, size, nature) and the performance evaluation metrics.
5. **Results:** the principal findings, then discussion, conclusions and references.

## Choosing a computer science project

Computer science projects are usually practical and often include building software. Sources of ideas are the student's own ideas, suggestions from supervisors, project lists on notice boards or the web, past years' projects and proposals from industry. Keep an open mind, and abandon a doubtful idea early. A project must have **significant computer science content**, not just an application where the main effort is in another area, and must be the **right size**: it should have an **achievable core with room for extension**. Ideas are refined into a project plan and submitted as a **project proposal**.

## The project format

Preliminary pages are the title page, certification or approval page, dedication, acknowledgement, table of contents, lists of tables, figures and symbols, and the **abstract**. **Chapter One (Introduction)** presents the background, problem, aim and objectives, and scope and limitations of the study.

## Chapter Two: the literature review

Chapter Two covers the **theoretical** literature and **related works**, built on the groundwork of others: present the basic theoretical concepts first, then review related works paragraph by paragraph. Existing solutions should be **recent** (for instance the last three years), with their drawbacks discussed.

- Give **due credit** to past authors and prefer **paraphrasing** to quotation, summarizing in your own words.
- Comment on past work and point out what it left out, but do not **derail into criticism**, which is unprofessional and unethical.

Its usual sub-headings are:

- **2.1 Conceptual framework:** the study's conceptual model and its linkages.
- **2.2 Theoretical framework:** the major theories and their relevance.
- **2.3 Empirical studies:** each study's purpose, sample and sampling technique, instruments, method of analysis and major findings.
- **2.4 Appraisal of reviewed literature:** a summary and the **gap in literature** that justifies the study, often with a summary table of related works.
