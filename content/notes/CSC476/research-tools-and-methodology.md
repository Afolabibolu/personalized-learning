# Research Tools and Methodology

## Quantitative tools

Quantitative methods collect objective, often numerical data with a design fixed before data collection, using standardized tools to control bias:

- **Observation**, recorded with an **observation checklist** of predetermined categories.
- **Questionnaires**, for structured interviews, self-administered offline or online collection, and telephone interviews, often with closed response lists.
- **Performance-based instruments**, where participants demonstrate a skill.
- **Diaries**, self-completed records of experiences.
- **Electronic data capture**, such as Google Forms.

## Qualitative tools

Qualitative research explores values, attitudes, opinions, feelings and behaviours, for example why households with bed nets do not use them. It uses **interviews**, **participant** and **non-participant observation**, **discussions** and **document review**. Using more than one method lets the researcher **triangulate** the data, adding rigour. Results are descriptive or explanatory rather than predictive.

## Questionnaires and sampling

Key issues for questionnaires are the target audience, the medium (paper or electronic), an acceptable response rate, anonymity and information about respondents. A questionnaire should be short and uncluttered. Question types include quantity, classification, multiple choice, **scale**, ranking, grid, **open-ended** ("What do you think about ...?") and **closed-ended** (choose one of the options). Questionnaires should be **pre-tested** before use.

In **probability sampling**, every member of the population has a known chance of selection, so the sample represents the population: it gives **less bias**, **better generalization** and lets researchers **estimate error**. **Non-probability sampling** relies on the researcher's judgment or convenience.

## Method and methodology

A **method** is an approach, procedure and guidelines used to conduct research, which may need tools and instruments. **Methodology** is the scientific study of the different ways research could be conducted: it compares alternative approaches, their feasibility and ethics, and explains **why** a particular approach was chosen and **how** it will be carried out. Baban (2009) classifies research by application (pure or applied), objectives (descriptive, exploratory, correlational, explanatory, analytical) and the information sought (positivist or phenomenological).

## Research instruments in computer science

An **instrument** is a tool that helps get to the output. For computer science researchers the most important are:

1. **Programs:** the most reliable tool to test and evaluate an application.
2. **Formal languages**, such as Z, to prove hypotheses mathematically.
3. **Systems**, evaluated in their context of use.
4. **Questionnaires**, to measure attributes of an application.

The choice of instruments must be justified.

## Framework or model?

A **model** is a schematic, often simplified, presentation of an existing or future situation, such as a process, workflow or life-cycle model; models are concrete, mathematical or computational. A **framework** lies between a model and a method: a structure, not completely detailed, for achieving a defined goal, often containing one or more models.
