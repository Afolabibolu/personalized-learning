# Plagiarism and Patents

## What plagiarism is

**Plagiarism** is taking someone else's expressions or thoughts, or one's own earlier work, without giving due credit, whether intentionally or by carelessness. The word comes from the Latin **plagiare**, "to kidnap". It is a serious academic offence that can lead to papers being retracted or rejected and damage an author's credibility.

## Types of plagiarism

1. **Intentional:** buying, borrowing or cut-copy-pasting others' work without credit.
2. **Unintentional:** wrong paraphrasing or improper citation.
3. **Plagiarism of ideas:** using others' ideas without credit.
4. **Plagiarism of text (direct, word-for-word):** copying text without credit.
5. **Mosaic (patchwork) plagiarism:** using the same words, phrases or paragraphs as the source instead of one's own.
6. **Self-plagiarism:** reusing one's own previously published work without citing it.

## Steps to plagiarism-free work

1. **Keep notes** of every source used: books, articles, websites, magazines and videos.
2. **Paraphrase or quote** correctly: a quote copies text word for word in quotation marks with a citation; a paraphrase restates the idea in your own words.
3. **Cite** the original author in the text and in the reference list, using one citation style consistently. Cite anything that is not your own, including your earlier papers; your own new results need no citation.
4. Check the work with a **plagiarism checker** before submission.

Reference managers such as **EndNote**, **Mendeley**, **RefWorks** and **BibTeX** help keep track of sources.

## Paraphrasing

A legitimate paraphrase does not just change or rearrange a few words. Read the source carefully, write the idea in your own words, compare your version with the original to make sure you have not used its exact words, and cite the source. Make clear at the start that the idea belongs to someone else, for example "According to Smith ...".

## Patents

A **patent** is an exclusive right granted for an innovation: a product or process that provides a new way of doing something or a new technological solution to a problem. In return, the applicant must disclose the details of the invention to the public. Protection lasts for a limited time, typically **20 years** from the filing date.

To obtain a patent, the inventor files an application with the country's **patent office**, communicating only through its forms. There are two ways to file:

- **Provisional specification:** optional and valid for only **one year**, within which the complete specification must be filed. It uses Form 1 (application for grant of patent), Form 2 (provisional or complete specification), Form 3 (foreign filing particulars) and Form 5 (declaration of inventorship).
- **Complete specification:** the full legal document for patent rights, valid for **20 years** from filing. It adds **Form 18**, the request for examination.
