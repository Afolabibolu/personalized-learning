# Transmission Media

The **transmission medium** (communication channel) carries messages between devices. Media are **guided** (wired) or **unguided** (wireless).

## Guided media

**Twisted pair cable** is the most common wire. Two identical wires are wrapped in a double helix, and the twisting reduces **crosstalk** (signal leaking from one wire to another) and external interference.

- **Unshielded twisted pair (UTP):** widely used in computers and telephones; no shielding, so it is cheaper but less protected.
- **Shielded twisted pair (STP):** a shield gives greater protection from crosstalk and interference, but it is heavier and costlier.

**Coaxial cable** has a solid wire core surrounded by foil or wire shields. The core carries the signal and the outer shield provides the ground. It is used for television signals and building security systems, and transmits better than twisted pair but costs more.

**Optical fibre** carries data as light through a thin glass or plastic fibre, at very high speeds, so it is widely used in Internet cables. It has three parts:

- the **core**, through which light travels;
- the **cladding**, which reflects light back into the core;
- the **sheath**, which protects the cable.

Light travels in one of these **propagation modes**:

- **Single mode:** a very thin core carries one ray almost straight, giving the least distortion, for long distances.
- **Multimode step-index:** the refractive index changes **abruptly** at the core-cladding boundary, so rays bounce at sharp angles and arrive at different times, distorting the signal.
- **Multimode graded-index:** the refractive index **decreases gradually** from the centre, so rays curve smoothly and arrive closer together, with less distortion.

## Unguided media

Signals propagate wirelessly through air, water or vacuum.

- **Microwave:** long-distance communication between parabolic antennas mounted on towers; the higher the tower, the greater the range.
- **Radio waves:** transmitter and receiver both use antennas, and radio gives mobility.
- **Infrared:** short-distance communication, used in TV remotes and wireless mice.

## Transmission impairments

Signals are imperfect when they arrive. The three causes of **impairment** are:

- **attenuation:** loss of energy over distance, corrected by amplifiers or repeaters;
- **distortion:** change of shape, because different frequency components travel at different speeds;
- **noise:** unwanted energy added by sources such as thermal noise, induced noise and crosstalk.
