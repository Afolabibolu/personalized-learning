# Waves on Strings, Standing Waves and Resonance

## Waves on strings

A transverse wave on a string under tension T with mass per unit length μ travels at **v = √(T/μ)**.

- For a wire of density ρ and cross-section A, **μ = ρA**. A copper wire of diameter 2.4 mm (ρ = 8920 kg/m³) holding 2 kg (T = 20 N) has μ = 0.0404 kg/m, so v = **22.3 m/s**.
- In a **hanging rope** the tension rises upward, because each point supports the load plus the rope below it. For a 3 m rope (0.05 kg/m) holding 4 kg, the tension at the ceiling is (4 + 0.15) × 10 = 41.5 N, so v = √(41.5/0.05) = **28.8 m/s**.

## Standing waves

Two equal waves travelling in **opposite** directions superpose to give a **standing (stationary) wave**: y = 2A sin kx cos ωt.

- **Nodes** never move; **antinodes** vibrate the most.
- Adjacent nodes are **λ/2** apart.
- Energy is not transported along a standing wave.

## Strings

For a string of length L fixed at both ends, the ends are nodes, so L = nλ/2 and

**fₙ = nv/(2L)**, for n = 1, 2, 3, ...

All harmonics are present. The **fundamental** is f₁ = v/2L, and the **first overtone** is the second harmonic.

## Air columns

- **Closed at one end** (a node at the closed end, an antinode at the open end): L = λ/4, 3λ/4, ..., so **fₙ = nv/(4L) with n odd only**. The **second overtone** is the 5th harmonic: for L = 0.80 m and v = 340 m/s, f = 5 × 340/3.2 = **531 Hz**.
- **Open at both ends**: fₙ = nv/(2L), with all harmonics.
- An **end correction** of about 0.6r is added at each open end.

## Resonance

When a body is driven at its **natural frequency** it vibrates with maximum amplitude: this is **resonance**. Examples are the resonance tube used to measure the speed of sound, and tuning instruments.

**Beats**: two notes of nearly equal frequencies f₁ and f₂ give a beat frequency **|f₁ − f₂|**.
