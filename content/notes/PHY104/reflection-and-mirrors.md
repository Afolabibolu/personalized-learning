# Reflection and Mirrors

## Reflection

**Laws of reflection**:

1. The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal lie in **one plane**.
2. **The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.**

## Plane mirrors

The image in a plane mirror is **virtual**, upright, the **same size** and laterally inverted. It lies **as far behind** the mirror as the object is in front.

- Rotating a mirror by θ turns the reflected ray through **2θ**.
- If a mirror rotates by 15° about the point of normal incidence, the image of an object 10 cm away moves on a circle of radius 10 cm. The object–image distance becomes 2 × 10 cos 15° = **19.3 cm**.

## Curved mirrors

A **concave (converging)** mirror brings parallel rays to a real **focus**. A **convex (diverging)** mirror spreads them from a virtual focus. For a spherical mirror the **focal length is half the radius of curvature: f = R/2**.

## Formulas

**1/u + 1/v = 1/f**, with **magnification m = v/u = image height/object height**.

Use the **real-is-positive** convention: real distances are positive and virtual ones negative, and a convex mirror has negative f.

**Example**: a concave mirror with R = 20 cm (f = 10 cm) gives a **virtual** image 4 times the object's size. With v = −4u, 1/u − 1/(4u) = 1/10, so **u = 7.5 cm and v = 30 cm** behind the mirror.

## Image types (concave)

- Object beyond C: real, inverted, diminished, between C and F.
- At C: real, inverted, same size.
- Between C and F: real, inverted, magnified, beyond C.
- **Inside F**: **virtual, upright, magnified** (the shaving mirror).

A **convex** mirror always gives a virtual, upright, diminished image, with a wide field of view, so it is used for driving mirrors.

**Example**: for an object 30 cm from a concave mirror of f = 10 cm, 1/v = 1/10 − 1/30, so v = 15 cm, real, and m = 0.5.
