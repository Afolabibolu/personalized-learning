# Wave Motion and the Wave Equation

## Waves

A **wave** is a disturbance that carries **energy** from place to place without carrying matter.

- In a **transverse** wave the particles vibrate **perpendicular** to the direction of travel (waves on strings, light).
- In a **longitudinal** wave they vibrate **along** it (sound).
- **Mechanical** waves need a medium; **electromagnetic** waves do not.

The key quantities are amplitude A, wavelength λ, frequency f, period T = 1/f and speed **v = fλ**.

## The progressive wave

A wave travelling in the +x direction is **y = A sin(kx − ωt)**, where:

- **k = 2π/λ** is the wave number;
- **ω = 2πf** is the angular frequency;
- the **wave speed** is **v = ω/k**.

**Example**: y = 4 sin π(0.01x − t), with x and y in cm. The vibration is along **y** and the propagation along **x**. k = 0.01π and ω = π, so **v = 100 cm/s**.

The **particle velocity** ∂y/∂t is different from the wave velocity. Its maximum is **Aω**: here 4π cm/s.

## The wave equation

Any function of the form **f(x ± vt)** that is single-valued and finite is a solution of

**∂²y/∂x² = (1/v²) ∂²y/∂t²**.

So y = 2x + 3t and y = 4/(5x − 3t)² are functions of x and t that travel. The first, though, grows without limit, so it is not a physical wave. y = 3 sin(πx − t²) is **not** a travelling wave, because t² is not linear in t.

## Wavefronts

A **wavefront** is a surface joining points in the same phase. Wavefronts are plane far from a source and spherical near a point source. **Huygens' principle**: every point on a wavefront acts as a source of **secondary wavelets**, and the new wavefront is the envelope of those wavelets. Huygens' construction explains reflection, refraction and diffraction.

The wave speed depends on the **medium**, not on the shape of the body carrying it: the speed of sound in a rod is √(Y/ρ), so it is constant along a rod of varying thickness.

Superposition: when waves overlap, the displacements add. This gives **interference** (constructive when in phase, destructive when out of phase) and **diffraction**, which is greatest when the opening is comparable with λ.
