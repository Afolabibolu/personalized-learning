# Nuclear Physics and Radioactivity

## The nucleus

A nuclide is written ᴬ_ZX, where Z is the atomic (proton) number and A the mass number. **Isotopes** have the same Z but different A.

The nuclear radius is **R = R₀A^(1/3)**, with R₀ ≈ 1.2 fm. For ⁶⁴Cu, R = 1.2 × 4 = **4.8 fm**. Nuclear density is roughly the same for all nuclei.

**Binding energy** is the energy needed to separate a nucleus into its nucleons. It equals the **mass defect** × c², with 1 u ≈ 931.5 MeV. The binding energy per nucleon peaks near iron. Heavy nuclei release energy by **fission** and light nuclei by **fusion**.

## Radioactive decay

Radioactivity depends **only on the nucleus**, not on the orbital electrons or chemistry.

- **α**: a ⁴₂He nucleus, so A falls by 4 and Z by 2. Strongly ionising, stopped by paper.
- **β⁻**: an electron from a neutron changing into a proton, so A is unchanged and Z rises by 1. Stopped by aluminium.
- **γ**: a photon, with no change in A or Z. Very penetrating.

When balancing equations, A and Z are both conserved. For ²³⁸₉₂U → X + 3α + β⁻, A = 238 − 12 = 226 and Z = 92 − 6 + 1 = 87.

## The decay law

**N = N₀e^(−λt)**, and the **half-life** is **T½ = ln 2/λ = 0.693/λ**. The half-life is a constant for each nuclide and **does not change** during decay. After n half-lives, **N = N₀/2ⁿ**: decaying away 63/64 leaves 1/64 = (½)⁶, which is **6 half-lives**.

Time to reach a fraction: **t = T½ ln(N₀/N)/ln 2**.

- 20% decayed with T½ = 30 days: t = 30 × ln(1/0.8)/0.693 = **9.7 days**.
- 6/10 of ²²⁶Ra decayed (T½ = 1620 y): t = 1620 × ln 2.5/0.693 = **2142 years**.

## Activity

**A = λN**, measured in becquerels (decays per second); the old unit was the curie. For 15 g of ²²⁶Ra, N = (15/226) × 6.02 × 10²³ = 4.0 × 10²² atoms and λ = 0.693/(1620 × 3.156 × 10⁷ s) = 1.36 × 10⁻¹¹ s⁻¹. That gives about 5.4 × 10¹¹ decays per second, or about **4.7 × 10¹⁶ per day**.

## Uses and safety

Radioisotopes are used for **carbon dating**, tracers, radiotherapy, sterilisation and thickness gauges. Shielding, distance and short exposure reduce the dose.
