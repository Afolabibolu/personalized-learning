# Refraction and Lenses

## Snell's law

The refractive index of a medium is **n = c/v**. At a boundary,

**n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂**.

Light bends **towards** the normal going into a denser medium and away from it going into a less dense one. Frequency stays the same, while speed and wavelength change.

**Parallel layers**: because n sin θ is the same at every boundary, the final angle depends only on the first and last media. Light from air at 35° passing through oil into water has sin r = sin 35°/1.33, so **r = 25.5°**. The oil layer does not matter.

## Total internal reflection

Going from a denser to a less dense medium, the **critical angle** c satisfies **sin c = n₂/n₁** (= 1/n into air). Beyond c, all the light is reflected. For glass with n = 1.5, c ≈ 41.8°. Uses include **optical fibres**, prism periscopes and binoculars.

**Real and apparent depth**: n = real depth/apparent depth, for viewing near the normal.

## Thin lenses

A **converging (convex)** lens brings parallel rays to a real focus; a **diverging (concave)** lens has a virtual focus.

- The **lens formula** (real-is-positive) is **1/u + 1/v = 1/f**, with **m = v/u**.
- The **power** is P = 1/f in dioptres (f in metres).
- The **lensmaker's formula** is 1/f = (n − 1)(1/R₁ + 1/R₂).

## Two-lens systems

Find the image of the first lens, then use it as the object for the second.

**Example**: lens C (f = 4 cm) at the 5 cm mark and lens D (f = 6 cm) at the 33 cm mark, with a pin at 0 cm.

- For C: u = 5, so v = 20 cm. The image is at the **25 cm mark**.
- For D: u = 33 − 25 = 8, so v = 24 cm. The image is at the **57 cm mark**.

For thin lenses in contact, **1/F = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂**.

## Instruments

- A **magnifying glass** uses an object inside f to give a virtual, magnified image.
- The **compound microscope** and the **astronomical telescope** (magnifying power f₀/fₑ) combine two converging lenses.

The **dispersion** of white light by a prism happens because n depends on wavelength.
