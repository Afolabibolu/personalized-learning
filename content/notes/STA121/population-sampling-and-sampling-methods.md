# Population, Sampling and Sampling Methods

## Basic ideas

Tasting a spoonful of soup to judge the pot is **sampling**: using information from a small quantity to make statements about a larger one.

- A **census** is a **complete enumeration** of every member of a population. It is costly, needs many resources, and its results come late.
- A **population** (or universe) is the group of units under study. It can be:
  - **finite**: countable, as with workers in a factory;
  - **infinite**: uncountable, as with stars in the sky;
  - **real**: all units physically present;
  - **hypothetical**: arising from repeated trials, such as tossing a coin again and again.
- A **sample** is a part of the population that should be **representative** of it.

**Sampling is unavoidable** when:

- the population is infinite;
- units are destroyed by testing;
- results are needed quickly;
- money or trained staff are limited;
- the population keeps changing;
- units are widely scattered.

## Errors

- A **sampling error** arises because a sample is only part of the population, so each sample gives a slightly different estimate.
- **Non-sampling errors** occur in censuses and surveys alike. Their sources are faulty planning, non-response, non-random selection, inaccurate returns and compiling errors.

A **parameter** (Greek letters: µ, σ) summarises a population and is **constant**. A **statistic** (Roman letters: x̄, s) summarises a sample and **varies from sample to sample**.

## Random sampling methods

- **Simple random sampling**: every unit has an equal chance. It can be with replacement (srswr) or without (srswor). All units must be available.
- **Stratified**: split a heterogeneous population into homogeneous **strata** and draw a simple random sample from each, in proportion to its size.
- **Cluster (area)**: divide the area into **clusters** using maps, then select clusters at random. It suits scattered populations, or populations with no full **sampling frame** (a complete list of the units).
- **Systematic**: pick a random start, then every **kth** unit; k is the **sampling interval**. It is quick, but a cycle in the list can bias it.
- **Multistage**: sample primary units, then units within them, in successive stages.

## Non-random methods

- **Quota**: the interviewer chooses respondents who meet set criteria. It is common in market research.
- **Judgment (purposive)**: the investigator picks the units they think are representative.
- **Convenience**: the units easiest to reach.

Inferences from non-random samples need great caution.
