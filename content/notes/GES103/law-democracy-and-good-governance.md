# Law, Democracy and Good Governance in Nigeria

## What law is

**Law** is a body of official rules and regulations, found in constitutions, legislation, judicial opinions and the like, used to govern a society and control the behaviour of its members (Ugiade 2004). **Black's Law Dictionary** (2004) calls it the regime that orders human activities and relations through the systematic application of the force of politically organised society. **Austin** (1832) defined law as "a rule laid down for the guidance of an intelligent being by an intelligent being having power over him": the command of the sovereign.

The 1999 Constitution states that **sovereignty belongs to the people of Nigeria**; that the **security and welfare of the people** are the primary responsibility of government; and that people's participation in government shall be ensured. It also lists **citizens' duties**: to abide by the Constitution, help enhance Nigeria's good name, respect the rights of others, contribute to their community, assist law enforcement, and declare income honestly and pay tax.

## Functions of law

1. **Maintaining public order:** preserving life and protecting property under the rule of law.
2. **Facilitating co-operative action:** contracts, associations and co-operation between tiers of government.
3. **Conferring legitimacy:** rules seen as made by right process pull people towards compliance. Law also sets out legitimate succession.
4. **Upholding rights and duties:** a right is a legally enforceable claim, a duty is a legal obligation, and the two are correlative.
5. **Communicating standards:** for example the right to fair hearing in Section 36.

**Law and morality.** Breaking a law brings sanctions; breaking a moral rule may not. Adultery is morally wrong in the South but not punishable, while in the North it is an offence under the Penal Code and Sharia law.

## Classification of law

- **Substantive law** defines rights and duties and prohibits wrongs, such as murder or robbery. **Procedural law** sets how cases are processed. Section 33 requires due process in criminal trials.
- **Public law** governs relations within government and between government and individuals: criminal, constitutional and administrative law. **Private law** governs relations between persons: contract, family, torts and land law.
- **Civil law** concerns private rights enforced by civil actions, often for damages. **Criminal law** defines crimes and penalties. No one may be convicted of an offence that is not defined in a written law.
- **Customary law** is the unwritten traditional law of a community, and it varies between groups. For inheritance, for example, the Yoruba use *idi-igi* or *ori-ojori*, and in Benin the eldest son inherits.

## Democracy and good governance

Black's Law Dictionary defines **democracy** as government by the people, directly or through elected representatives. It needs **rule of law**, guaranteed fundamental rights and a free press. The chapter sees **good governance** as the welfare of the people: people instituted the state to secure their freedom and welfare. Nigeria's legitimacy crisis is attributed to the state defaulting on that social contract.
