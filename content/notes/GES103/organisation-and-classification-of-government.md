# Organisation and Classification of Government

## Organs of government

Modern states have two primary purposes: to keep order and to promote citizens' welfare. Government works through three organs, or arms:
- **rule making**, by the legislature;
- **rule application**, by the executive;
- **rule adjudication**, by the judiciary.

How separate or fused these organs are, under the doctrine of **separation of powers**, shapes how a government is organised.

## Aristotle's classification

Aristotle classified governments by **who rules** and **in whose interest**:

| Who governs | Good form (in the interest of all) | Perverted form (in the interest of self or few) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One | Monarchy | Tyranny |
| A few | Aristocracy | Oligarchy |
| Many | Polity | Democracy |

- **Monarchy** is rule by a king or queen, either absolute or constitutional, as in Jordan, England and Morocco. It can decay into **tyranny**; the book's examples are Idi Amin and Sani Abacha.
- **Aristocracy**, from *aristos*, "the best", can decay into **oligarchy**.
- **Polity**, constitutional democracy, was Aristotle's best form. It can decay into "democracy", which he saw as the worst.

Akindele and others call the corrupt form of democracy **mobocracy**: rule by a violent, disorderly mob.

## Distribution of power

- **Unitary:** one central legislature and government. Local units exist at its mercy. It suits homogeneous, small countries such as Ghana, Britain, France and Kenya.
- **Confederal:** a league of sovereign states with a weak centre, which is prone to secession. The 13 US states under the Articles of Confederation (1781–1789) are an example.
- **Federal:** the constitution divides power between levels that are independent and co-ordinate, in Wheare's definition. Examples are Nigeria (36 states, 774 local governments and the FCT), the USA, Brazil and India. Its weakness is over-centralisation.

## The executive

- **Presidential:** the president is head of state and of government, and commander-in-chief. The most common form, as in the USA and Nigeria.
- **Parliamentary (cabinet, Westminster):** a ceremonial head of state and a prime minister drawn from the majority party. It has **collective responsibility** and **fusion of powers**. It began in Britain.
- **Dual executive (mixed):** a president and a prime minister, as in France.

## Ideology

- **Capitalism:** private ownership of the means of production, with multi-party politics, as in the USA, Canada and Japan.
- **Socialism:** collective, state ownership for the benefit of all.
- **Communism:** an advanced stage of socialism, from Marx and Engels, aiming at a classless society. In practice it was often authoritarian.

## Other typologies

Arend Lijphart classified democracies by elite behaviour and the structure of society: **consociational**, de-politicised, centripetal and centrifugal. Other forms of government include fascism, feudalism, theocracy, totalitarianism, colonialism and militarism.
