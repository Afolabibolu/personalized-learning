# ICT and the Social Sciences in the 21st Century

## Natural and social sciences

**Science** is the application of systematic methods to obtain knowledge, together with the knowledge those methods produce. Ancient explanations of the world mixed observation with magic and superstition; science developed from human curiosity.

- The **natural sciences** explain and predict events in the natural environment. They divide into fields such as biology (botany, zoology), geology (mineralogy, geomorphology), chemistry (organic, inorganic) and physics (biophysics, quantum mechanics). Each examines a "slice" of nature.
- The **social sciences** examine human relationships. Just as nature has ordered relationships that must be discovered through controlled observation, so does the social world. They divide into anthropology, economics, political science, psychology and sociology.

Because every discipline involves human beings, the chapter argues that all disciplines "rest on the oars of social sciences".

## What ICT is

**Information and communications technology (ICT)** was introduced in the early 1990s to replace the term information technology (IT), in recognition of the computer's communicating abilities. It covers computers, communications equipment and their services. It is also seen as a **skill for life**, like literacy: something everyone needs to live confidently in modern society.

ICT has three places in education:
- **learning about ICT**, as a subject;
- **learning with ICT**, as a medium that helps instruction;
- **learning through ICT**, where it is an essential tool without which a course cannot be taught.

## ICT in the social sciences

ICT has become a learning tool, a platform for interaction between groups, and a form of communication that is becoming a universal language. Many jobs, especially in banking, now depend on computer skills. Political scientists use computers to analyse and even predict **voting behaviour**, and **card reader machines** in Nigerian elections are ICT-based.

**ICT literacy.** Alvin Toffler said the illiterate of the 21st century will be those who cannot learn, unlearn and relearn. Haddad and Jurich (2002) named four basic issues in using ICT in education: **effectiveness, cost, equity and sustainability**.

## ICT in teaching and learning

ICT affects the teacher, the learner and the image of the social sciences:

- **Curricula** are shifting from content to competency and performance. They need varied information sources, student-centred, inquiry-based and problem-centred learning, authentic settings, and teachers as coaches and mentors rather than content experts.
- **Information literacy**, "the capacity to identify an issue and then to identify, locate and evaluate relevant information", is now one of the generic skills graduates need, alongside reasoning, problem solving, communication and teamwork.
- **Independent learning:** ICT moves education from teacher-directed to student-centred models, such as problem-based learning and the Web as an information source. Students take responsibility for their own learning and can learn anywhere; distance is no barrier.
- **Learning theories:** ICT has arrived alongside **constructivist** theories, which hold that learning is achieved by actively constructing knowledge.
