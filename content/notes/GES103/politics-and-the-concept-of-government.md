# The Study of Politics and the Concept of Government

## Political science and politics

Political science is a social science: the systematic, scientific study of politics, people's political behaviour and political relationships. Aristotle (384–322 BC) called it the "master science", because almost everything happens in a political context. It became a distinct academic discipline only in the last quarter of the 19th century.

Politics has no single definition:
- **Aristotle:** two or more people interacting are in a political relationship. This is too broad.
- **Harold Lasswell:** "who gets what, when and how", which centres on power.
- **David Easton:** "the authoritative allocation of values for a society". This is too abstract.
- As the working of government institutions. This is too narrow, because it ignores parties and pressure groups.

What most agree on is that politics involves **power, influence and authority**. Political scientists are not politicians: they analyse politics calmly and objectively and are sceptical of power. Billy Dudley (1975) listed politics as consciousness formation, social mobilisation, contestation, institutional struggle and transcendence.

Before 1950, philosophers, theologians and historians dominated the study of politics. The **behavioural revolution** of the 1950s then shifted attention to human behaviour and integrated the social sciences. Politics matters because "man is a political animal": if we do not take part, others decide for us.

## Scope and levels

The field covers comparative politics, international relations, public administration and political theory. Politics happens at three levels:
- **state politics**, the most important level, with federal, state and local government;
- **intra-state politics**, within groups in the state;
- **inter-state (international) politics**, which has no institution with a monopoly of force. The UN, for example, cannot compel obedience.

## Power, influence and authority

**Power** is A getting B to do what B would not otherwise do, through force, domination or manipulation; propaganda is a form of manipulation. **Influence** works without sanctions or force. It can be manifest (explicit) or potential (implicit). **Authority** is a superior form of power that people accept as rightful. It can be **traditional**, resting on custom such as a monarch, or **charismatic**, resting on personal qualities, as with Awolowo, Nkrumah or Mandela.

## The state and government

The **state** is a territorial organisation with a legitimate monopoly of force. **Government** is its administrative apparatus. Theories of the state's origin:
- **divine**: ordained by God;
- **natural or organic**: the state grows naturally;
- **force**: conquest, "might is right";
- **genetic**: family to tribe to state;
- **social contract**: Hobbes, Locke and Rousseau.

The elements of the modern state are a **population** and a **defined territory**, its physical elements, and a **functional government** and **sovereignty**, its spiritual elements.
