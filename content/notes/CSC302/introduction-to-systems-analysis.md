# Introduction to Systems Analysis and Design

## Information technology and information systems

**Information technology (IT)** is the combination of hardware, software and services that people use to manage, communicate and share information. An **information system (IS)** combines information technology, people and data to support business requirements. **Systems analysis and design** is a step-by-step process for developing high-quality information systems.

A **system** is a set of related components that produces specific results; a **mission-critical** system is vital to a company's operations. **Data** is the raw material; **information** is data transformed into output that is valuable to users. An information system has five key components:

1. **Hardware**;
2. **Software** (system and application software);
3. **Data**;
4. **Processes** (the tasks and business functions);
5. **People** (stakeholders and users).

## How business uses information systems

- Systems were once grouped as office, operational, decision support and executive systems.
- **Enterprise computing systems** support company-wide operations, such as ERP.
- **Transaction processing systems (TPS)** handle large amounts of data, are mission-critical and process transactions as a group.
- **Business support systems** provide job-related information at all levels, such as management information systems, RFID and what-if analysis.
- Knowledge management and user productivity systems complete the set.

Information system users range across top managers, middle managers and knowledge workers, supervisors and team leaders, and operational employees.

## Systems development methods

- **Structured analysis** uses the **systems development life cycle (SDLC)**. It is a predictive approach and process-centred, describing the system graphically with process models, and it follows the **waterfall model**. Its phases are systems planning (a systems request and preliminary investigation, including a feasibility study), systems analysis, systems design, systems implementation, and support and security.
- **Object-oriented analysis** combines data and the processes that act on it into **objects**. It usually follows phases like the SDLC, but is more interactive.
- **Agile methods** are the newest. They emphasise continuous feedback and iterative development, as set out in the Agile Manifesto; Scrum and Extreme Programming (XP) are examples, and the spiral model is related.
- Other methods include **JAD**, **RAD**, the Rational Unified Process (RUP) and the Microsoft Solutions Framework.

## Guidelines and the analyst

Five guidelines for systems development:

1. develop a project plan;
2. involve users and listen carefully;
3. use project management tools to track tasks and milestones;
4. develop accurate cost and benefit information;
5. remain flexible.

The **IT department** covers application development, systems support and security, user support, database administration, network administration, web support and quality assurance. A **systems analyst** translates business requirements into IT projects. The role needs solid technical knowledge, strong oral and written communication, analytic ability and an understanding of business operations; certification is an important credential.
