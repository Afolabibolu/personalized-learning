# Requirements Modelling and Fact-Finding

## The systems analysis phase

**Systems analysis** is the second of five phases of the SDLC. Its objective is to understand the proposed project, ensure that it will support business requirements, and build a solid foundation for systems design. Its activities are:

1. **requirements modelling** (outputs, inputs, processes, performance and security);
2. **data and process modelling**;
3. **object modelling**;
4. considering **development strategies**.

The analyst needs analytical and interpersonal skills.

## Team-oriented methods

- **Joint application development (JAD):** users, managers and IT staff work together in sessions led by a project leader acting as facilitator, with a recorder documenting results. JAD can give a more accurate statement of requirements and a stronger commitment to the system, but it is more expensive and cumbersome if the group is too large.
- **Rapid application development (RAD):** a team-based method that speeds up development through four phases: requirements planning, user design, construction and cutover. Its objective is to cut time and expense by involving users in every phase. It can produce systems quickly, but may neglect the company's strategic business needs and quality standards.
- **Agile methods:** develop a system incrementally through frequent deliverables. In **Scrum** (a rugby term), the "pigs" (product owner, facilitator and development team) are committed, while the "chickens" (users, stakeholders and managers) are involved. Agile is flexible with change and reduces risk, but needs highly skilled team members and may suffer large changes in scope.

## System requirements checklist

- **Outputs:** for example, a daily inventory report sorted by part number.
- **Inputs:** for example, employees swiping ID cards to record labour costs.
- **Processes:** for example, calculating GPA at the end of each semester.
- **Performance:** for example, supporting 25 simultaneous users with a response time under four seconds.
- **Controls:** for example, logon security at the operating-system and application levels.

A **scalable** system offers a better return on the initial investment. **Total cost of ownership** estimates tend to understate indirect costs.

## Fact-finding

Decide what information you need, then plan. Ask **who, what, where, when, how and why**, separating what is done from what should be done.

**Interviews** follow seven steps:

1. determine the people to interview;
2. establish objectives;
3. develop questions, avoiding leading questions and using open-ended, closed-ended and range-of-response questions;
4. prepare, keeping to no more than an hour;
5. conduct the interview, listening actively and summarising;
6. document it promptly;
7. evaluate it, watching for biases.

Other techniques are:

- **document review**;
- **observation** (beware the **Hawthorne effect**: people behave differently when watched);
- **questionnaires and surveys**;
- **sampling** (systematic, stratified or random);
- **research** and site visits;
- **brainstorming**.
