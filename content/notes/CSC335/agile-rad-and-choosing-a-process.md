# Agile Methods, RAD and Choosing a Process

## Agile methods

**Agile methods** emphasise flexibility in producing software quickly and capably. The **agile manifesto** values:

- **individuals and interactions** over process and tools;
- **working software** over comprehensive documentation;
- **customer collaboration** over contract negotiation;
- **responding to change** over following a plan.

Agile holds that every project needs to be handled differently, so methods are tailored to each project. Tasks are divided into **time boxes** (small time frames) to deliver specific features. Each iteration involves cross-functional teams working at once on planning, requirements analysis, design, coding, unit testing and acceptance testing. At the end, a working product is shown to the customer. Each **build is incremental**, and the final build holds all the features.

**Merits:**

- a realistic approach;
- promotes teamwork and cross-training;
- functionality is developed and demonstrated rapidly;
- minimal resources;
- suits fixed or changing requirements;
- delivers early partial solutions;
- minimal rules and documentation;
- easy to manage;
- flexible for developers.

**Demerits:**

- not suitable for complex dependencies;
- more risk to sustainability, maintainability and extensibility;
- needs an overall plan and an agile leader;
- depends heavily on a clear customer;
- high dependence on individuals, since documentation is minimal;
- hard to transfer knowledge to new members.

## Rapid application development (RAD)

**RAD** was a response to plan-driven waterfall processes such as **SSADM**. It is also called **adaptive software development**. It is an incremental prototyping approach with **minimal planning in favour of rapid prototyping**, gathering requirements through **workshops or focus groups**. Functional modules are developed **in parallel as prototypes** and integrated into the product, by small teams of developers, domain experts and customer representatives. The prototypes must be **reusable**. RAD suits software driven by **user-interface** requirements, so GUI builders are called RAD tools.

**When to use RAD:**

- the system can be modularised for incremental delivery;
- designers for modelling are available;
- the budget allows automated code-generation tools;
- domain experts are available;
- requirements change and prototypes are shown every 2–3 months.

**Advantages:** changing requirements accommodated, measurable progress, short iterations, productivity with fewer people, reduced development time, reusable components, early integration and encouraged feedback.

**Disadvantages:** dependence on strong, highly skilled team members, only modular systems can be built, high dependence on modelling skills, and costly for cheap projects. Success often comes at the cost of overruns.

## Factors in choosing a software process

1. customer involvement;
2. stability of requirements;
3. team size and proximity;
4. developer experience;
5. familiarity with the technology;
6. familiarity with the domain.
