# The Software Process and Life Cycle

## What a process is

A **process** is a series of steps involving activities, constraints and resources that produce an intended output of some kind. It uses a set of tools and techniques. A process:

- prescribes all its major activities;
- uses resources, subject to constraints such as a schedule;
- produces intermediate and final products;
- may be made of sub-processes, linked or in a hierarchy;
- has entry and exit criteria for each activity;
- organises activities in sequence, so timing is clear;
- has guiding principles, including the goal of each activity.

Processes **impose consistency and structure** on activities, **guide us** to understand, control, examine and improve them, and **capture experience** to pass on.

**Reasons for modelling a process:**

1. to form a common understanding;
2. to find inconsistencies, redundancies and omissions;
3. to find and evaluate activities for reaching the process goals;
4. to tailor a general process for a particular situation.

## The software development cycle

- **Requirements analysis and specification:** eliciting, analysing, specifying and validating requirements.
- **Design:** defining the architecture, components and interfaces, in preliminary and detailed design.
- **Implementation:** programming and debugging, component implementation and integration, and documenting.
- **Testing:** an empirical investigation that informs stakeholders about quality, through **unit, integration and system testing**.
- **Installation and acceptance testing.**
- **Maintenance:** cost-effective support after delivery, including bug reporting and fixing, changed requirements and upgrades.

## The software life cycle

When a process builds software, it is called the **software life cycle**:

1. requirements analysis and definition;
2. system (architecture) design;
3. program (detailed or procedural) design;
4. writing programs (coding);
5. testing: unit, integration and system;
6. system delivery (deployment);
7. maintenance.

## Software process models

The main models are:

- the **waterfall** model;
- the **V** model;
- **prototyping**;
- **phased development** (increments and iterations);
- the **spiral** model;
- **agile** methods;
- **rapid application development (RAD)**.

Process modelling uses tools and techniques that show the activities, their order, and the products and resources involved.
