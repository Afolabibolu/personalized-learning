# Ethics and Social Responsibility

## Ethics

**Ethics** studies **what is right and wrong in human behaviour**: the principles, values and standards that guide decisions. It appeals to **conscience**, not only law or religion: **a person may obey the law and still act unethically**, or act ethically where the law is silent. Key terms include virtue, integrity, morality, values, **utilitarianism**, **deontology**, justice, transparency, accountability, duty and stakeholders.

## Ethics in entrepreneurship

Business decisions affect employees, consumers, investors, communities and the environment. The core principles are **honesty, integrity, fairness, respect, transparency and accountability**. Ethics builds trust, protects reputation, supports sustainability and attracts loyalty. **Ethical entrepreneurship means being legally compliant and morally sound.**

## Issues and dilemmas

An **ethical issue** arises when decisions affect stakeholders in ways that challenge moral standards, or when values conflict. Common issues are **corruption and bribery**, fraud, product safety, insider trading, exploitation, discrimination, environmental harm and **false advertising**. An **ethical dilemma** is a choice between **two or more conflicting moral options**, where choosing one may violate another principle, often with no clear right answer.

Four approaches help resolve dilemmas:

- **Utilitarian**: what produces the **greatest good for the greatest number**?
- **Rights**: which option best protects the rights of those affected?
- **Justice**: is the decision fair and impartial?
- **Virtue or duty**: what would a person of good character do?

The steps are: recognise the dilemma, identify stakeholders, gather facts, evaluate options and act, then reflect.

## Social responsibility

**Social responsibility** means **balancing profit with positive impact on society**; it turns values into visible action. **Corporate social responsibility (CSR)** is social responsibility in organised business practice. Examples are recycling and tree planting, fair wages and safe workplaces, scholarships, honest advertising, and paying taxes and creating jobs.

**The 3Ps** measure responsible success: **People** (fair wages, safety, inclusion), **Planet** (recycling, clean energy, sustainability) and **Profit** (ethical growth and long-term stability). The **Dangote Group** illustrates them with scholarships and hospitals, waste heat recovery, and industrial jobs.

**Carroll's pyramid of CSR** has four levels: **economic** (be profitable), **legal** (obey laws), **ethical** (do what is fair) and **philanthropic** (voluntary contributions). CSR enhances reputation, attracts talent and builds goodwill, but faces high costs, weak commitment, conflict between profit and social goals, and public scepticism about **greenwashing**.

Ethics answers "what is the right thing to do?"; social responsibility answers "how do we contribute to the good of society?".
