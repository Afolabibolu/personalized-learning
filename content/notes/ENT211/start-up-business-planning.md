# Start-up Business Planning

## The business plan

A **business plan** is a comprehensive document that serves as a **roadmap** for establishing and growing a business: a formal statement of the company's **objectives and how it intends to achieve them**, which also builds **investor confidence**. **Business planning** is the **ongoing, dynamic process** of thinking, analysing and adapting that roadmap.

Its components are:

- **executive summary**;
- **company description** (the problem, solution and competitive advantage);
- **market analysis** (target market, trends, competitors and **SWOT**);
- **products and services**;
- **organisation and management**;
- **marketing and sales strategy**;
- **financial projections**; and
- **funding request**.

A plan **attracts funding**, gives a roadmap, **minimises risk**, improves decisions, communicates the vision and attracts talent. The **steps** to prepare one are: identify your objective and audience, set up the outline, review it, write the plan, and have it reviewed and **updated regularly**. Key questions ask about the market, product, customers, competition, team, pricing, risks, channels, **break-even** and financial needs.

## Business concept development

Feasibility may show a "gap in the market"; concept development tests whether there is a **"market in the gap"**. A **business concept** clearly defines a new business idea and is a **bridge between the idea and the full plan**. The process is brainstorming, customer research, estimating market potential, **prototyping**, devising a marketing strategy, **test marketing** and evaluating results.

## Pitching

A **pitch** is a face-to-face selling activity for an idea, product or capability. An **elevator pitch** lasts **30 to 60 seconds**, the length of an elevator ride. A great pitch needs a deep understanding of the business, persuasive communication, **proof of concept** and a competitive edge; **storytelling** matters because decisions are often based on perceptions.

## Business models

A **business model** describes **how an organisation creates, delivers and captures value**. A good one is **aligned to goals**, **self-reinforcing** and **robust** against **imitation, holdup, slack and substitution**. The **Business Model Canvas** (Osterwalder and Pigneur, 2010) has **nine elements**: key partners, key activities, key resources, **value propositions**, customer relationships, channels, **customer segments**, **cost structure** and **revenue streams**.

A **lean business plan** is a simplified, concise version in bullet points, suited to start-ups that must adapt quickly. A **pitch deck** follows the **10/20/30 rule**: **10 slides**, **20 minutes** and at least **30-point font**.
