# Legal Issues in Entrepreneurship

## Business law

**Business law** is the branch of law that **governs business organisations and commercial transactions**. Its branches include company law, **contracts**, tort, employment law, **intellectual property**, **taxation** and alternative dispute resolution. It helps entrepreneurs **mitigate risks**, provides **dispute resolution**, protects businesses, employees and consumers, and guarantees order. Its **sources in Nigeria** are the **Constitution**, **legislation**, **case law**, **received English law**, **customary law** and **international law**. A key statute is the **Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA) 2020**.

## Business structures

- **Sole proprietorship**: one individual trader, simple and cheap to set up with little formality; a business name must be **registered before trading**.
- **Partnership**: persons carrying on business in common with a view to profit, with a **maximum of twenty members** (legal and accounting firms are exempt); the death of a partner does not end it.
- **Incorporated companies**: **limited by shares**, **limited by guarantee**, or **unlimited**.
- **Start-ups**, under their own legal framework.

## IP and contracts

**Intellectual property rights** are exclusive rights over **creations of the mind**. To prevent disputes from verbal agreements, entrepreneurs should use written **service agreements**, **non-disclosure agreements**, **founder agreements** (equity, profit sharing, roles and dissolution agreed from the start), **investment agreements** and dispute resolution agreements.

## Employment, data and licences

Employment is regulated by the **Labour Act**, the **Pension Reform Act**, the Nigerian Tax Act and the **Employees' Compensation Act**; terms should be in a **signed written contract**. Data is regulated by the **Nigeria Data Protection Act** and the **GAID 2025**, so businesses need clear privacy policies. Sector licences come from bodies such as the **Corporate Affairs Commission**, **SEC**, **NAFDAC** (food and drugs), the **Central Bank of Nigeria** and the **Nigerian Communications Commission**. **Corporate governance** sets rules for meetings, records and keeping **business and personal funds separate**.

## Tax and insurance

A **tax** is a charge imposed by government to raise money for a public purpose; it is **purely statutory**. Taxes are paid to the **Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS)** or a **State Inland Revenue Service**, and every business must obtain a **Tax Identification Number (TIN)**. Under the **Nigeria Tax Act 2025**, small companies with an annual turnover of **₦50 million or less** and fixed assets of ₦250 million or less are **exempt from companies' income tax, capital gains tax and the development levy**. **Insurance** spreads losses fairly and encourages risky but viable ventures; types include liability, property, workers' compensation, health, business interruption and goods-in-transit.

## Start-ups

The **Nigerian Start-up Act 2022** defines a start-up as a **limited liability company up to 10 years old** whose objects include innovation in **digital technology**. Labelled start-ups must report annually and keep proper accounts. The **Economic Development Incentive** replaced Pioneer Status, offering qualifying start-ups a **three-year tax holiday** that can be extended by two years.
