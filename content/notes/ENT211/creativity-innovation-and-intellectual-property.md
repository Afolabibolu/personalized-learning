# Creativity, Innovation and Intellectual Property

## The sequence

**Creativity** is the ability to **generate new ideas**; **innovation** is the process of **turning creative ideas into practical solutions**, often by improving or recombining existing ones; and **entrepreneurship** turns those solutions into viable products in the **market**. So: **creativity (idea) → innovation (development) → entrepreneurship (market)**. Creativity is the thinking aspect and innovation the doing aspect.

Creativity is a **necessity for survival** and **can be taught**. Rigid thinking (paradigms) blocks it. The **right brain** is lateral and imaginative; the **left brain** is logical and evaluates market potential; successful entrepreneurs use both. Individuals boost creativity by staying open-minded, observing other industries, keeping an **idea diary** and taking breaks. Organisations do it by making creativity a core value, **hiring diverse people**, setting expectations and **rewarding creativity**, as Google does.

## The creative process

The **seven steps** are: **preparation**, **investigation**, **transformation**, **incubation** (letting ideas "marinate"), **illumination** (the "Eureka" moment), **verification** (testing feasibility and cost) and **implementation**. Techniques include **brainstorming** (open, non-judgmental idea generation), **mind mapping**, **TRIZ** (systematic problem-solving) and **rapid prototyping**.

**Barriers** (mental locks) include searching for one "right" answer, being overly logical, blindly following rules, over-specialisation, fear of looking foolish or of failure, and believing "I am not creative".

## Types of innovation

- **Incremental**: small, continuous improvements, such as bottled drinks to cans.
- **Breakthrough (radical)**: new products or technologies with high risk and investment, such as electric cars.
- **Disruptive**: challenges norms and creates new markets, as **Uber** did to taxis and **Netflix** to Blockbuster.
- **Architectural**: existing technology used to create new markets or customers.

## Innovation management

**Innovation management** is a systematic approach to generating, developing and implementing new ideas that create value and competitive advantage. Methods include **innovation mapping**, **hackathons**, **design thinking**, **design sprints**, **agile thinking**, **Blue Ocean Strategy** (capturing unexplored markets) and **Jobs to Be Done**. Innovation is rewarded with recognition, bonuses or promotions. **Portfolio management** aligns innovation projects with strategy.

## Intellectual property

**Intellectual property (IP)** is **creations of the mind** protected by law:

- **Patents**: exclusive rights for inventions, in return for disclosing the details.
- **Copyright**: books, music, films and software.
- **Trademarks**: signs that distinguish one business's goods from another's.
- **Industrial designs**: a product's appearance.
- **Geographical indications**: goods linked to a place.
- **Trade secrets**: confidential business information.
