# The Design–Reality Gap

## What the model is

Big data projects in developing countries face many barriers that have rarely been measured systematically. The **design–reality gap model**, an ICT for Development (ICT4D) framework, measures them.

- It records the gap between the **design requirements or assumptions** of a project (for example big data) and the **current reality** on the ground.
- The gap is scored from **0** (no gap: everything needed is present) to **10** (radical gap: none of the requirements is present).
- It is measured along the seven **ITPOSMO** dimensions.

## The ITPOSMO dimensions

1. **Information:** the information the design needs versus the information really used.
2. **Technology:** the technology the design requires versus what exists now.
3. **Processes:** the work processes needed versus those carried out now.
4. **Objectives and values:** what stakeholders need to want versus what they really want.
5. **Staffing and skills:** the staff numbers and skills required versus those available.
6. **Management systems and structures:** those required versus those in place.
7. **Other resources:** the time and money required versus what is available.

## The Colombian example

Applied to big data in the Colombian public sector, the model found serious barriers on all seven dimensions:

| Dimension | Gap | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information | 7 | Some variety of data, but little volume or velocity |
| Technology | 6 | Good mobile, poor sensors, a strong digital divide |
| Processes | 7 | Few information value-chain processes |
| Objectives and values | 7 | Basic data policies, but no big data culture |
| Skills and knowledge | 7 | Foundational, not specialised |
| Management | 7 | General IT structures only |
| Other resources | 5 | Some budgets earmarked for big data |

The actions proposed were greater use of existing datasets, investment in data-capture technologies, and prioritising value generation over data generation.

## Collecting and using the data

Primary data comes from **structured interviews** and from **group discussions**. Each dimension is rated 0 to 10; the ratings are **summed** and interpreted against a guideline table. In one e-government case, the total of **35** meant partial failure that could become total unless gaps were closed. The largest gap was management systems and structures (8), followed by staffing and skills and objectives and values (7 each).

In a city e-government project, the gaps (no ICT department, five of nine IT staff hired) led to an action plan to create the department and hire staff, so the model proved a useful **mid-implementation** tool.

## Importance to e-government managers

The model:
- focuses management attention on key project issues;
- digs beyond technical issues to human and organisational factors;
- gives a systematic, credible basis for reporting.

It helps explain why so many IT projects in low-income countries fail totally or partially.
