# PowerPoint and Effective Presentations

## What PowerPoint is

**PowerPoint** is a **presentation graphics program** for professional presentations as on-screen slide shows, **speaker's notes** and **audience handouts**; it can also prepare posters and leaflets.

## Planning

Planning improves a presentation's quality, makes it more effective and saves time. Decide the **purpose**, the **type** of presentation, the **audience** and its needs, and the **time available**; a rule of thumb is **about one slide per minute**.

## Building slides

A new presentation starts with a **Title Slide** layout. Its dotted boxes are **placeholders**, regions reserved for text or graphics; the default title font is **Calibri at 44 pt**. **New Slide** (**Ctrl + M**) adds a **Title and Content** slide, and **Layout** changes it, for example to **Two Content** with a picture placeholder, which resizes large images to fit. Content icons insert a table, chart, **SmartArt** (such as an organisation chart), picture or video, and inserting a picture shows the **Picture Tools Format** contextual tab. **Themes** on the **Design** tab give a professional look; light text on dark or dark on light helps accessibility. To change something on **every** slide, edit the **Slide Master**. Save often with **Ctrl + S**; files get the **.pptx** extension.

## Design rules

- Give **every slide a title**, and make headings larger than subheadings.
- Use **bulleted lists** for key points and **numbered steps** for sequences, with **parallel phrasing**.
- Follow the **6 × 6 rule**: **six or fewer items per slide** and **six or fewer words per phrase**; omit unnecessary words.
- Use **30 to 48 pt for titles** and **24 to 28 pt for text**, and **avoid all capitals**.
- Make sure the **background contrasts with the text**; avoid too many colours, clutter and fancy effects; embrace empty space.
- Keep images clear and relevant, **label figures and tables**, and put **units** on charts.
- **Use animation sparingly**, and use the same transition each time.
- **Proofread**: PowerPoint checks both ordinary spelling and **contextual spelling** ("Whose house" versus "Who's house").

The classic error is writing sentences on slides and reading them: **less is more**.

## Views and delivery

Views include **Normal** (slide thumbnails and the slide, with notes below), **Slide Sorter** (all slides as thumbnails, for **rearranging by drag and drop**), **Reading**, **Notes Page** and **Outline**. **Slide Show** view presents to the audience; advance with the **Spacebar**, a **left click** or the **right arrow**. Slides can be **deleted** or **hidden**. **Transitions** control how slides appear, and **animations** control how text and objects appear. **Speaker notes**, in the notes pane, remind the speaker what to say and can be printed.

Rehearse, face the audience rather than the screen, test the room, and have a **back-up**: a USB copy, an emailed copy and printed slides.
