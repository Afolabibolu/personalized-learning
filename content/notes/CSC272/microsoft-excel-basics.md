# Microsoft Excel Basics

## Workbook, worksheet and cell

- A **workbook** is the Excel file, a collection of one or more **worksheets**. Sheets can be added, deleted, hidden and reordered, and a workbook can also hold **chart sheets**.
- A **worksheet** is made of individual **cells**, each holding a value, a formula or text, plus an invisible draw layer for charts and images.
- A **cell** is the smallest but most powerful part of a spreadsheet. It is identified by its **cell address**, the **column letter followed by the row number**: the cell in column C, row 11 is **C11**.

## The Excel window

- The **active cell** is the selected cell, highlighted by a box; double-click it or press **F2** to edit it.
- A **column** is a vertical set of cells; a worksheet has **16,384 columns**, labelled **A to XFD**. A **row** is a horizontal set; there are **1,048,576 rows**.
- The **fill handle** is the small dot at the lower right of the active cell, used to fill series.
- The **address bar (Name Box)** shows the active cell's address, and the **formula bar** shows the active cell's content and lets you enter formulas.

## Worksheet tabs

Right-click a sheet tab to **Rename** it (or double-click it), **Insert** a new sheet (which appears to the left), change its **Tab Colour**, or **Delete** it. **Undo does not reverse deleting a worksheet.**

## Entering data

Excel accepts text, numbers (which can display as **currency** or percentages), dates and formulas. **By default text aligns left, while numbers, dates and formulas align right.** Finish an entry with **Enter** (moves **down** one cell), **Tab** (moves one cell **right**), an arrow key or the tick on the formula bar. To edit, double-click the cell or use the formula bar; to delete, press **Delete**.

## Mouse pointers and autofill

The pointer's shape shows what it will do: a **white cross** selects cells, a **four-headed arrow** on a cell border **moves** its contents, and a **black + at the bottom right** is the **autofill** handle. Autofill extends series such as **Jan → Feb, Mar**, Mon → Tue, or 1, 2, 3 → 4, 5, 6; a value that is not part of a series is simply copied. When a formula is autofilled, its **cell references change**, because Excel uses **relative cell referencing** by default.

## Formatting

The **Alignment group** changes alignment and text direction; **Wrap Text** shows text on several lines in a cell; and **Merge and Centre** joins cells into one and centres the data, keeping **only the upper-left cell's data**. **Format as Table** styles data, and the **Number group** formats numbers as currency (such as ₦), percentages or dates.
