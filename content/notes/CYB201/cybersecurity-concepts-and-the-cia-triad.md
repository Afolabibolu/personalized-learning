# Cybersecurity Concepts and the CIA Triad

## Cyber and cyberspace

"**Cyber**" describes anything involving computers, the internet or virtual environments, as in cybersecurity, cyberspace and cybercrime. **Cyberspace** is the **digital environment where information is generated, processed, stored and exchanged through interconnected computer systems and communication networks**, including the devices and infrastructure that let people share information across locations. The **Internet** is the physical infrastructure of cables and servers, while cyberspace is the virtual "place" where digital interactions such as social media, online gaming and e-commerce occur.

## Cybersecurity

The word splits into **cyber**, the technologies (systems, networks, software, data), and **security**, safeguarding them. **Cybersecurity is the practice of safeguarding computers, digital systems, networks and software applications against unauthorized intrusions and malicious activities**: the body of technologies, processes and practices designed to protect networks, computers, programs and data from attack, damage or unauthorized access. It is "primarily about **people, processes and technologies** working together".

It matters because cyber attacks are **extremely expensive** for businesses, a data breach causes **reputational damage**, attacks are increasingly sophisticated and destructive, and regulations such as the **GDPR** force organisations to protect personal data.

## The CIA triad

The **CIA triad** is the foundation of cybersecurity: the core objectives that must be achieved to protect data and systems.

- **Confidentiality**: protecting sensitive information so that **only authorised individuals** can access it, and keeping their identities private. It is compromised by cracking poorly encrypted data, **man-in-the-middle (MITM) attacks** and disclosure. Standard measures: **data encryption** (turning plaintext into unreadable **ciphertext** that only the right key can decrypt), **two-factor authentication** and **biometric verification**.
- **Integrity**: preserving the **correctness and dependability** of information, so it is not modified, removed or tampered with by unauthorised parties. Techniques include hashing and digital signatures, and standard measures are **uninterruptible power supplies**, **file permissions** and **cryptographic checksums**.
- **Availability**: ensuring systems, networks and data are **accessible to legitimate users whenever they are needed**, protecting them from equipment failure, cyber threats or natural disasters. Standard measures are **routine system maintenance** and **well-prepared disaster recovery strategies**.

The course's basic concepts also include **authentication**, **access control**, **non-repudiation** (a party cannot deny an action it took) and **fault tolerance** (continuing to work despite failures).
