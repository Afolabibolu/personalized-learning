# Risk Management and the Impact of Cyber Attacks

## Risk management

**Risk** is the likelihood of suffering harm or loss: an action, event or natural occurrence that could cause an undesirable outcome. **Risk management** is essentially to **look at what could go wrong and then decide on ways to prevent or minimise those problems**. It has **three processes**:

1. **Risk assessment**: identifying threats to information or information systems, determining the likelihood of their occurrence, and identifying system vulnerabilities that could be exploited.
2. **Risk mitigation**: taking steps to avoid or reduce risk to acceptable levels.
3. **Evaluation and assessment**: reviewing how well the measures work and repeating the cycle as the environment changes.

The steps include analysing risks to data and resources, prioritising them, putting controls in place and monitoring them. Good cybersecurity management also improves an organisation's reputation with customers and partners, and it requires technology and management to work together.

## Disaster recovery

**Disaster recovery** plans how to restore systems and data after a major failure, attack or natural disaster. Organisations should **maintain regular backups** of critical systems and data, **store them in multiple locations** to avoid single points of failure, and integrate recovery procedures into their cybersecurity strategy, so that **business continuity** is maintained during and after incidents.

## Impact of cyber attacks

- **On systems**: data loss or corruption (ransomware), **system downtime** (DoS and malware), and compromised integrity, as when ransomware locks a hospital's patient records.
- **On networks**: traffic disruption by **DDoS** attacks, intrusions that steal data in transit, and fast-spreading worms.
- **On organisations**: **financial loss** (response, legal fees, compensation, restoration), **reputation damage**, and **regulatory penalties** under laws such as GDPR and HIPAA. The 2017 **WannaCry** attack cancelled thousands of UK NHS appointments.
- **On civil institutions**: disruption of public services (healthcare, utilities, transport), **erosion of public trust**, disruption of **democratic processes**, and economic damage.

Civil institutions face **resource and funding constraints** (over 60% of IT leaders in such sectors think funding is insufficient), **insider threats** (insiders may be twice as likely to cause damage as outsiders) and the **human factor**: human error is a primary cause of breaches, so training and awareness are essential.
