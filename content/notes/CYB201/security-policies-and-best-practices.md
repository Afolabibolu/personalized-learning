# Security Policies and Best Practices

## Why policies matter

**Security policies** outline the **standards, rules and procedures** that regulate how systems are used and how sensitive information is protected; with best practices, they are the foundation of an effective cybersecurity approach. Their importance: they **protect sensitive data**, **establish security standards**, **reduce cyber risks** such as hacking, phishing and malware, **guide user behaviour** (reducing human error), ensure **legal and regulatory compliance**, **improve incident response**, **enhance access control**, **promote accountability**, **support business continuity** and **build trust and reputation**.

## Types of security policy

- **Enterprise Security Policy (ESP)**: a **high-level, strategic framework** for the organisation's whole cybersecurity approach, setting objectives and vision. It addresses governance and overall risk management, covers all users, departments and systems, and defines roles, responsibilities and compliance requirements. Example: all organisational data must be protected according to regulatory standards, with management responsible.
- **Issue-Specific Security Policy (ISSP)**: detailed rules for **specific security concerns**, such as password usage, email and internet access, mobile devices or social media. Example: a password policy requiring complex passwords changed every 60 days.
- **System-Specific Security Policy (SysSP)**: technical guidance for **securing individual systems**, mainly for IT staff and administrators: server configurations, access control, network devices, monitoring and logging. Example: all database servers must encrypt stored data and log all access attempts.
- **Supplemental policies**: a **network security policy** (firewalls, VPNs, Wi-Fi), a **data security policy** (encryption, backups, access control) and an **acceptable use policy (AUP)**, which defines what users can or cannot do with organisational resources.

## Best current practices

1. **Data protection and encryption**: encrypt sensitive data **in transit and at rest**, and back up critical data securely.
2. **Access control and least privilege**, using role-based access control and regular reviews.
3. **Strong password management** and **MFA** for sensitive accounts.
4. **Network security**: firewalls, **intrusion detection and prevention systems (IDS/IPS)**, network segmentation, and secure protocols (HTTPS, VPNs, SSH).
5. **Software updates and patch management**: apply patches promptly and remove unsupported software.
6. **Security audits and vulnerability assessments**, including penetration testing.
7. **Security awareness and training** on phishing, social engineering, safe browsing and incident reporting.
8. **Incident response planning** and **backup and disaster recovery planning**, with backups in several locations.
