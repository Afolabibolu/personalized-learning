# Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection and Radiation

## Conduction

Heat flows through a slab of area A and thickness L at the rate

**H = kA ΔT/L**,

where k is the **thermal conductivity** (W m⁻¹ K⁻¹). Metals are good conductors; wood and air are poor ones.

**Example**: a board 30 mm thick passing 0.0086 W/cm² (86 W/m²) with ΔT = 20 °C has k = 86 × 0.03/20 = **0.13 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹**.

**Composite slabs** in series carry the **same heat current**. Rubber (k = 0.13) and cardboard (k = 0.03), each 2 mm thick, with faces at 0 °C and 25 °C: 0.13(θ − 0) = 0.03(25 − θ) gives the interface temperature **θ ≈ 4.7 °C**.

## Convection

**Convection** is heat carried by the bulk movement of a fluid: warm, less dense fluid rises and cooler fluid sinks. It happens **only in liquids and gases**, never in solids. Examples are sea breezes, room heating and kettles.

## Radiation

Every body above **0 K** emits **electromagnetic radiation**, mostly infrared at ordinary temperatures. It needs no medium.

The **Stefan–Boltzmann law**: the power radiated is

**P = eσAT⁴**, with σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴,

where e (0 to 1) is the **emissivity**. It depends on emissivity, **surface area** and the fourth power of the **absolute** temperature, but not on length.

An **ideal absorber (blackbody)** has **e = 1**: it absorbs all radiation falling on it and is also the best emitter. A body at temperature T in surroundings at T₀ has net radiation **eσA(T⁴ − T₀⁴)**.

**Examples**:

- The earth's surface at 15 °C (288 K) as a blackbody radiates σT⁴ = **390 W/m²**.
- A blackbody at 500 °C (773 K) radiates twice as much at T = 773 × 2^(1/4) = 919 K = **646 °C**.
- Skin at 33 °C in a room at 20 °C loses σ(306⁴ − 293⁴) ≈ 79 W/m² ≈ **7.9 mW/cm²**.
