# The First Law and Thermodynamic Processes

## The first law

**Q = ΔU + W**: heat supplied to a system equals the rise in its **internal energy** plus the **work done by** the system. Q is positive when heat flows in, and W is positive when the system **expands** and does work on its surroundings.

Internal energy is a **state function**; work and heat depend on the **path**. A **cyclic process** begins and ends at the **same state**, so ΔU = 0 and the net work equals the **area enclosed** by the loop on a P–V diagram.

## Work

**W = ∫P dV**, the area under the P–V curve.

- **Isobaric** (constant P): **W = PΔV**. 1000 J in at 2 × 10⁴ Pa with ΔV = 0.025 m³ gives W = 500 J, so **ΔU = +500 J**. At 2.5 kPa from 1 to 3 m³ with 12.5 kJ in, W = 5 kJ and **ΔU = 7.5 kJ**. The final temperature is 300 × 3 = **900 K**.
- **Isochoric** (constant V): W = 0, so Q = ΔU.
- **Isothermal** (constant T): ΔU = 0 for an ideal gas, so **Q = W = nRT ln(V_f/V_i)**. Heat flows **in** during an expansion. From 3 L at 20 atm to 24 L: W = PV ln 8 = 2026 × 3 × 2.079 ≈ **12.6 kJ**.
- **Adiabatic** (no heat flow, Q = 0): **PV^γ = constant** and TV^(γ−1) = constant, so W = −ΔU. For γ = 1.40 from 5 atm and 12 m³ to 30 m³, P = 5(12/30)^1.4 = **1.39 atm**. A monatomic gas (γ = 5/3) doubling its volume drops to 2^(−5/3) = 0.315 of its pressure: 101 kPa becomes **31.8 kPa**.

In an **adiabatic free expansion** into a vacuum, no work is done and no heat flows, so **U stays constant**.

## Polytropic processes

For PVⁿ = C, W = (P₁V₁ − P₂V₂)/(n − 1). For an insulated gas expanding with PV² = C and U falling by 100 kJ, the work done by the gas is **+100 kJ**.

## Heat capacities of gases

**C_p − C_v = R** (per mole), and **γ = C_p/C_v**: 5/3 for monatomic and about 1.4 for diatomic gases.

Without work being done on it, a gas cannot be **compressed** (isobarically, isothermally or adiabatically), but it can expand adiabatically.
