# Kinetic Theory and Ideal Gases

## Ideal gases

The gas laws combine into **PV = nRT = Nk_BT**, with **R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹**, k_B = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K and N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. Temperatures must be in **kelvin**.

- **Charles**: at constant P, V ∝ T. 4.0 m³ at 320 K becomes **5.0 m³** at 400 K.
- **Boyle**: at constant T, PV is constant. 4.0 m³ at 2 bar becomes **20 m³** at 0.4 bar.
- Number of molecules: N = PV/(k_BT). A 6000 m³ room at 20 °C and 101 kPa holds about **1.5 × 10²⁹** molecules. (None of the typed paper's options matches.)
- Molar mass M = mRT/(PV): 2.71 g occupying 1.29 L at 18 °C and 765 mmHg gives M ≈ **50 g/mol**.

## Kinetic theory

The model assumes:

- many molecules in random motion;
- negligible molecular volume;
- perfectly elastic collisions;
- no forces between molecules except during collisions.

Collisions with the walls give **P = ⅓ρc̄²** = (1/3)(N/V)m⟨c²⟩.

If the volume rises at constant temperature, molecules strike the walls **less often**, so the pressure falls. If the temperature rises at constant volume, they move faster and strike **harder and more often**.

## Temperature and energy

The average kinetic energy per molecule is **⟨½mc²⟩ = (3/2)k_BT**, so the **kinetic temperature** is T = m⟨c²⟩/(3k_B).

At 37 °C (310 K), the average energy is 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 310 = **6.42 × 10⁻²¹ J**. Doubling it from 10 °C (283 K) needs 566 K = **293 °C**. Molecular motion would stop only at **absolute zero**.

From P = nk_BT, the number density is n ∝ P/T. Going from (60 Pa, 400 K) to (30 Pa, 300 K) gives **2n/3**.

## Speeds

**c_rms = √⟨c²⟩ = √(3RT/M) = √(3k_BT/m)**. For a set of speeds, square them, average, then take the root. For 15 molecules with speeds 6, 9, 10, 13, 13, 15, 17, 17, 18, 19, 20, 20, 22, 23 and 24 m/s, c_rms ≈ **17.2 m/s**. The most probable speed is lower than the mean speed, which is lower than c_rms.
