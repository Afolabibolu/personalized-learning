# Temperature and Thermometry

## Heat and equilibrium

**Heat** is energy transferred from one body to another because of a temperature difference, and it always flows from **hotter to colder**. Bodies are in **thermal contact** if they can exchange energy. They are in **thermal equilibrium** when that exchange stops, which means they are at the **same temperature**.

Touch is unreliable: a metal tray and a plastic bottle from the same freezer are at the same temperature, but the metal feels colder because it conducts heat away faster.

## The zeroth law

If two bodies A and B are each in thermal equilibrium with a third body C, then **A and B are in thermal equilibrium with each other**. C acts as the thermometer, so the zeroth law is what makes thermometers possible. A mercury-in-glass thermometer works on this law.

**Temperature is a scalar** described by a number.

## Thermometers

Each type of thermometer uses a **thermometric property** that changes with temperature:

- the length of a liquid column (mercury-in-glass);
- the pressure of a gas at **constant volume**, or the volume of a gas at **constant pressure**;
- the electrical resistance of a conductor (resistance thermometer);
- the e.m.f. of a **thermocouple**;
- the colour of a hot body (pyrometer).

## Scales

- **Kelvin (thermodynamic)**: the ice point is 273.15 K and the **triple point of water** (ice, water and vapour in equilibrium) is defined as **273.16 K**. **0 K = −273.15 °C** is **absolute zero**.
- **Celsius**: ice point 0 °C and steam point 100 °C. **T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15**, so a temperature **difference** is the same in K and °C (450 °C difference = 450 K).
- **Fahrenheit**: ice point 32 °F and steam point 212 °F, with 180 divisions. **T_F = (9/5)T_C + 32**, so 65 °F = 18.3 °C ≈ **291 K**.

For any **linear scale** with fixed points, the reading is proportional to the fraction of the way from the lower to the upper fixed point. On a scale X where water freezes at −170 °X and boils at −53.5 °X, 340 K (66.85 °C) reads −170 + 0.6685 × 116.5 ≈ **−92 °X**.
