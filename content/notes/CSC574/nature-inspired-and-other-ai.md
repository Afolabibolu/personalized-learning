# Neural Networks, Genetic Algorithms and Other AI Technologies

## What the topic is

Besides rule-based expert systems, intelligent systems use techniques inspired by nature and by human language: neural networks, evolutionary computation, swarm methods, intelligent agents and natural language processing. Many modern solutions combine them as hybrids.

## Subtopics

**Artificial neural networks.** Neural networks are networks that learn and can perform tasks that are hard for conventional computers, such as playing chess or recognising faces. They use patterns rather than the If-Then-Else rules of expert systems, building a model from inputs and outputs, and suit poorly structured problems.

Neural networks are the oldest AI technology. McCulloch and Pitts laid the foundations in 1943, Rosenblatt's Perceptron (1957) was the first working AI system, and Rumelhart's backpropagation revived the field. Three network types:
- **MLP (multilayer perceptron):** when the categories are known.
- **SOM (self-organising or Kohonen map):** learns without a teacher.
- **Hopfield network:** for optimisation.

Uses include bankruptcy prediction, credit rating, investment analysis and target marketing. Visa's network-based credit authorisation could cut fraudulent transactions by as much as 40%. A limitation is that their knowledge is hidden and hard to explain.

**Genetic algorithms and evolutionary computing.** Genetic algorithms solve optimisation and search problems such as jet engine design, portfolios and network design. Their techniques are **selection** (survival of the fittest), **crossover** and **mutation**. They combine random search (crossover and mutation) with goal-oriented search (maximising a fitness function), on many parallel paths at once.

**Other biologically inspired methods.** Ant colony optimisation models how simple ants together find efficient paths: the colony's intelligence emerges from simple rules and communication. Artificial immune systems model how the body learns to defend against intruders.

**Intelligent agents.** Small programs that work in the background, with these characteristics:
- adaptability
- autonomy
- collaborative behaviour
- a human-like interface
- mobility
- reactivity

Types include shopping and information agents, personal agents, data-mining agents, and monitoring and surveillance agents.

**Natural language processing.** NLP lets users communicate with computers in their own language. Its categories are interfaces to databases, machine translation, text scanning and indexing, text generation, and speech systems. Products still cannot hold human-like dialogue.

**Hybrid systems.** Examples are neuro-fuzzy systems, expert systems that use fuzzy logic, and genetic algorithms that choose the best neural network structure.

## Key terms

- **Fitness function:** what a genetic algorithm maximises.
- **Supervised vs unsupervised learning:** learning with or without a teacher's labels.

## Exam-style point

"Researchers look to nature for inspiration: what broad categories?" Give neural networks (the brain), evolutionary computation and genetic algorithms (evolution), swarm methods such as ant colony optimisation (social insects) and artificial immune systems (the immune system).
