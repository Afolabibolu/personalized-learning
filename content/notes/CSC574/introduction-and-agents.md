# Introduction to Intelligent Systems and Agents

## What the topic is

This course aims to show different views of AI and how to apply AI algorithms to real-world problems. One working definition: AI is "the study of how to make agents that do things at which, for the moment, people are better" (adapted from Rich and Knight, 1991). An intelligent system is built around an **agent** that senses its environment and acts in it.

## Subtopics

**Four approaches to AI.**
- **Thinking humanly:** cognitive modelling.
- **Acting humanly:** Turing.
- **Thinking rationally:** the logicians.
- **Acting rationally:** achieving the best outcome.

**Schools of thought.** Turing defined intelligent behaviour as human-level performance in cognitive tasks, enough to fool an interrogator. Minsky defined intelligence in terms of mechanisms ("a human is a 'meat' machine"). More recently some scientists see intelligence as an evolutionary process: the more competitive the environment, the more intelligent. Swarm intelligence is another view.

**The Turing test.** A human interrogator communicates by teletype and the computer passes if the interrogator cannot tell whether a human or a computer is at the other end. Keeping communication to terminals makes the test objective. In 1946 Turing predicted that by 2000 a computer conversing for five minutes would have a 30% chance of passing as human.

**Applications.** Everyday uses include games, medicine, finance, the web and robotics. Industry uses include remote diagnostics, clinical guidelines, business rules, data mining, natural language and product selection. Research problems include medical knowledge representation, game players that learn from scratch, robot taxi drivers and natural language understanding.

**The agent model.** An agent acts in an environment: it receives percepts through **sensors** and acts through **actuators**, judged by a **performance measure**. A **rational agent** selects the action that maximises performance given its percept sequence and knowledge base. A learning agent improves with experience.

**Properties of environments.**
- **Observability:** full or partial.
- **Uncertainty:** deterministic or stochastic.
- **Experience:** episodic or sequential.
- **Change:** static or dynamic.
- **Continuity:** discrete or continuous.
- single or multi-agent.

**AI and computational intelligence.** The AI community imitates human thinking methods, with rule-based expert systems as a typical result. The computational-intelligence community models natural systems: neural networks, swarm intelligence, evolutionary computation and fuzzy systems.

## Key terms

- **Percept / action:** what an agent senses and what it does.
- **Rational agent:** chooses actions that maximise expected performance.

## Exam-style point

Past papers ask whether intelligent robots will displace humans. Frame the answer with the agent model: robots excel in well-defined, observable, deterministic environments; humans keep the advantage in partially observable, dynamic and multi-agent settings.
