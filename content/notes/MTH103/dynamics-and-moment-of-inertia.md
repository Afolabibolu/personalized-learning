# Dynamics, Relative and Circular Motion, and Moment of Inertia

## Forces and Newton's laws

The **resultant** of several forces is their vector sum, and **F = ma** gives the acceleration. Forces of 15 N and 10 N at right angles give a resultant at **33.69°** to the 15 N force.

**Driving force − resistance = ma**. A 400 kg motorcycle accelerating at 5 m/s² against 120 N of resistance needs 2000 + 120 = **2120 N**.

**Momentum** is mv, and the **impulse** Ft equals the change in momentum. A 0.5 kg body whose velocity changes from (5i − 2j) to (25i + 58j) in 10 s has F = 0.5(20i + 60j)/10 = **i + 3j N**.

**Work** is F·d, and **power** is the rate of doing work, F·v. (These formulas, Newton's laws and Hooke's law below come from general knowledge, alongside the scanned slides.)

## Relative motion

The position of B relative to A is **r_AB = r_B − r_A**. The relative velocity is **v_AB = dr_AB/dt = v_B − v_A**, and the relative acceleration is a_AB = dv_AB/dt.

## Circular motion

For angle θ(t), the **angular velocity** is **ω = dθ/dt** and the **angular acceleration** is α = dω/dt. With arc s = rθ, the speed is **v = rω**.

The acceleration has two components:

- **tangential**: r dω/dt;
- **normal (radial, inward)**: **v²/r = rω²**.

In **uniform circular motion** ω is constant, so only a = v²/r remains.

**Example**: r = 2 m and θ = t² give ω = 2t, v = 4t, a normal component of 8t² and a tangential component of 4.

The velocity of a point on a rotating rigid body is **v = ω × r**.

## Elastic strings

**Hooke's law**: the tension is **T = λx/l**, where λ is the modulus of elasticity, l the natural length and x the extension. A 0.3 kg mass hanging on a string of natural length 0.8 m stretched to 1.3 m gives λ = mgl/x = 3 × 0.8/0.5 = **4.8 N** (with g = 10).

## Moment of inertia

For particles, **I = Σmᵢrᵢ²**, and the **radius of gyration** k satisfies **k² = I/M**.

- For a thin rod about one end, I = ∫₀ˡ ρx² dx = **Ml²/3**, so k = l/√3. A rod of length 3 cm with density 5 kg/cm has I = 45 kg cm².
- For a rectangular plate about an axis through its centre, **I = Md²/12**, so k = d/√12.

**Parallel axes theorem**: **I = I_G + Mh²**, where I_G is about the parallel axis through the centre of mass and h is the distance between the axes.
