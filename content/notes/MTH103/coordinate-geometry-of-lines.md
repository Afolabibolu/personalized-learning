# Coordinate Geometry of Lines

## Distance and division

By Pythagoras, the distance from A(x₁, y₁) to B(x₂, y₂) is **|AB| = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)**. The point dividing AB in the ratio **m : n** is

**x = (mx₂ + nx₁)/(m + n), y = (my₂ + ny₁)/(m + n)**.

When m = n this gives the **midpoint ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)**.

**Example**: the ends (p − q, p + q) and (p + q, p − q) have midpoint **(p, p)** and length √((2q)² + (2q)²) = **2q√2**.

## Slope and equations

The **slope (gradient)** is **m = tan θ = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁)**, where θ is the angle with the positive x-axis. A line is fixed by two points, or by one point and its slope: **y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)**. It can be written in several forms:

- **intercept form** x/a + y/b = 1;
- **general form** ax + by + c = 0;
- **slope-intercept form** y = mx + c.

For 5y + 2x + 7 = 0, the slope is −2/5 and the x-intercept is −7/2.

Points are **collinear** if the slopes between them are equal: A(5, 6), P(x, y) and B(2, 3) give (y − 3)/(x − 2) = 1, so **x = y − 1**.

## Parallel and perpendicular lines

Parallel lines have **equal slopes**. Perpendicular lines satisfy **m₁m₂ = −1**.

The **locus** of points equidistant from A and B is the perpendicular bisector of AB. For A(5, 3) and B(7, 4), equating squared distances gives **4x + 2y = 31**.

## Distances and angles

The perpendicular distance from P(x₁, y₁) to the line ax + by + c = 0 is

**d = |ax₁ + by₁ + c| / √(a² + b²)**,

and from the origin it is |c|/√(a² + b²). For the point (9, 5) and 2x − y − 4 = 0, d = |18 − 5 − 4|/√5 = **9/√5**.

The angle θ between lines with slopes m₁ and m₂ satisfies **tan θ = |(m₂ − m₁)/(1 + m₁m₂)|**.
