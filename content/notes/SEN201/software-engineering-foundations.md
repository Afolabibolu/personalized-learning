# Software Engineering as a Discipline

## What software engineering is

Software is more than program code: it includes all the **documentation and configuration data** needed to make programs operate correctly. Engineering is developing products using well-defined scientific principles and methods. **Software engineering** is therefore the engineering branch concerned with developing software products using well-defined scientific principles, methods and procedures. The **IEEE** defines it as **"the application of a systematic, disciplined, quantifiable approach to the development, operation and maintenance of software."** Software engineering is part of the computing disciplines alongside Computer Science, Information Technology, Information Systems and Computer Engineering, and bridges theory and real-world applications.

The name was proposed in **1969 at a NATO conference** on what was called the **software crisis**: large systems were released late, did not deliver what users needed, cost more than expected and were unreliable. Techniques such as structured programming, information hiding and object-oriented development followed in the 1970s and 1980s.

A small program can be written without these principles, but large products need them. Software engineering reduces complexity with two techniques: **abstraction**, simplifying a problem by omitting irrelevant details, and **decomposition**, dividing a problem into smaller problems solved one by one.

## Why it is needed

The need arises from the **high rate of change** in user requirements and environments, and from: **large software**; **scalability** (without engineering, re-creating software would be easier than scaling it); **cost**; the **dynamic nature** of software; and **quality management**.

## Products and quality

There are two kinds of software product: **generic products**, stand-alone systems sold on the open market to any customer (such as PC software), and **customised (bespoke) products**, commissioned by a particular customer. The essential attributes of good software are:

- **Maintainability**: it can evolve to meet changing needs, since change is inevitable.
- **Dependability and security**: reliability, security and safety.
- **Efficiency**: no wasteful use of memory or processor cycles.
- **Acceptability**: understandable, usable and compatible for its users.

Types of application include stand-alone applications, interactive transaction-based applications, **embedded control systems** (probably the most numerous), batch processing, entertainment, modelling and simulation, data collection systems and systems of systems. Four **fundamental principles** apply to all: use a **managed and understood process**; **dependability and performance** matter; **understand and manage the specification and requirements**; and make **effective use of existing resources** through reuse.
