# Agile Development and Extreme Programming

## Why agile?

Businesses operate in a **global, rapidly changing environment** and must respond to new opportunities, markets and competitors. Requirements inevitably change, because customers cannot predict how a system will affect their work. Processes that fully specify the requirements before designing, building and testing are not geared to rapid development. For **safety-critical control systems**, where complete analysis is essential, a **plan-driven** approach is right; in a fast-moving business environment, rapid processes are needed.

**Agile methods** are **incremental development methods with small increments**: new releases are typically made available to customers **every two or three weeks**, customers are involved to get rapid feedback, and documentation is minimised in favour of informal communication.

## The principles of agile methods

1. **Customer involvement**: customers are closely involved throughout, providing and prioritising requirements and evaluating iterations.
2. **Incremental delivery**: the customer specifies the requirements for each increment.
3. **People not process**: the team's skills are recognised, and members develop their own ways of working.
4. **Embrace change**: expect requirements to change and design to accommodate them.
5. **Maintain simplicity**: in the software and the process, actively eliminating complexity.

## Challenges

Customer involvement depends on a customer willing and able to spend time with the team; team members may not suit intense involvement; **prioritising changes** is hard with many stakeholders; **maintaining simplicity needs extra work** under delivery pressure; and large organisations with defined-process cultures find informal processes hard to adopt.

## Plan-driven or agile?

Most projects mix both. The choice depends on technical, human and organisational questions: whether a detailed specification is needed first; whether rapid feedback through incremental delivery is realistic; **system size** (agile works best with a **small, co-located team**); the type of system (real-time systems need more design); expected lifetime; available tools; team organisation (distributed or outsourced teams need design documents); culture; team skill; and **external regulation**, which requires detailed documentation.

## Extreme Programming (XP)

In **XP**, requirements are expressed as **scenarios called user stories**, implemented directly as tasks. **Programmers work in pairs** and **write tests for each task before writing the code**. New versions may be built **several times a day** and releases go to customers **roughly every two weeks**. A new build is accepted only if **all existing automated tests and the new tests pass**. Customers are intimately involved in specifying and prioritising requirements. Because incremental development tends to degrade structure, XP emphasises constant refactoring.
