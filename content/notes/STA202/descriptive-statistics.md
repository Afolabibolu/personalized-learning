# Descriptive Statistics

## Central tendency

- The **arithmetic mean** is x̄ = Σx/n. If 30 observations have mean 15, their sum is **30 × 15 = 450**.
- The **median** is the middle value of the ordered data (the average of the two middle values when n is even). For 250, 320, 240, 210, 260, 330, 310, 260, the ordered data are 210, 240, 250, 260, 260, 310, 320, 330, so the median is **(260 + 260)/2 = 260**.
- The **mode** is the most frequent value.

The **combined mean** of two groups is **(n1x̄1 + n2x̄2)/(n1 + n2)**. With 650 employees earning ₦2,750 and 700 earning ₦2,500, the combined mean is (650 × 2750 + 700 × 2500)/1350 ≈ **₦2,620**.

## Position

The median, **quartiles**, **deciles** and **percentiles** are **averages of position**: they divide ordered data into 2, 4, 10 and 100 equal parts. Q1, Q2 (the median) and Q3 are the quartiles.

## Dispersion

- The **range** is the largest value minus the smallest.
- The **interquartile range** is Q3 − Q1, and the **quartile deviation (semi-interquartile range)** is **(Q3 − Q1)/2**; an interquartile range of 24 gives a quartile deviation of **12**.
- The sample **variance** is **s² = Σ(x − x̄)²/(n − 1)**, and the **standard deviation** s is its square root. For grouped data, use Σf(x − x̄)²/(Σf − 1).

The variance measures the average squared distance from the mean; the standard deviation is in the same units as the data.

## Graphs

**Bar diagrams** represent grouped and ungrouped data with bars of equal width. A **histogram** shows a frequency distribution of continuous data with adjoining bars. A **stem-and-leaf display**, used in exploratory analysis, splits each value into a **stem (the leading digits)** and a **leaf (the trailing digit)**, keeping the raw data visible. **Scatterplots** show the relationship between two variables.

## Skewness

In a **symmetrical** distribution, **mean = median = mode**. In a **positively (right) skewed** distribution, with a long tail to the right, **mean > median**; in a **negatively skewed** one, **mean < median**. The mean is pulled towards the tail by extreme values, while the median is resistant to them.
