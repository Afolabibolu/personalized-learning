# Introduction to Human-Computer Interaction

## What HCI is

**Human-computer interaction (HCI)** is "the **multidisciplinary field** concerned with the **design, evaluation and implementation of interactive computing systems for human use**, and the study of the phenomena surrounding that use" (Dix, Finlay, Abowd and Beale, 2004). We use it every day: at an ATM, unlocking a phone with a fingerprint, booking a flight or asking a smart speaker for music. The **interface** is the **point of contact** (screen, keyboard, touch surface, microphone); the **interaction** is the **ongoing exchange of actions and responses** over time. HCI is not just "making things look nice": it is equally about **error prevention, learnability and accessibility**.

- **UI (user interface)**: the visual, auditory or physical elements through which a user communicates with a system, **what you see and touch**.
- **UX (user experience)**: the broader impression formed while using a product, including emotions and satisfaction, **how you feel**.
- **HCI**: the overarching field studying both, plus the cognitive and organisational factors, **the science behind both**.

## History

- **1940s to 1950s**: machines like **ENIAC** were operated through plugboards and switches.
- **1950s to 1970s**: punched cards and teletypes gave the **command-line interface (CLI)**, with memorised syntax.
- **Pioneers**: **Vannevar Bush's Memex**, an early vision of hypertext; **Ivan Sutherland's Sketchpad**, which pioneered **direct manipulation** with a light pen; and **Douglas Engelbart's NLS**, whose "Mother of All Demos" introduced hypertext linking and the **first computer mouse**.
- **GUI era**: **Xerox PARC's Alto and Star** introduced **WIMP** (windows, icons, menus, pointer) and the desktop metaphor; Apple's **1984 Macintosh** commercialised it and Windows followed.
- **Web era**: hyperlink navigation; then **multi-touch**, popularised by the **2007 iPhone**; and now **natural interaction**: voice, gesture and AR/VR.

Newer is not always better: CLIs remain faster for many expert tasks. HCI emerged as a distinct field in the **early 1980s**; ACM founded **SIGCHI in 1982** and held the first **CHI conference in 1983**.

## Why it matters

**Usability** (ease of learning and use), **productivity**, **safety** (confusing medical displays have caused accidents), **accessibility** (an ethical and often legal duty) and **business value** (usability raises conversion and cuts support costs; Amazon's one-click ordering removed a friction point). In Nigeria, **USSD banking** such as *737# succeeds because it works on cheap feature phones without internet: design for context.

## Five components

1. **User**: the person, with their age, expertise, culture and ability.
2. **System**: hardware and software, with inputs and outputs.
3. **Interaction**: the two-way exchange of input and feedback.
4. **Task**: the user's goal.
5. **Context**: the physical, social and organisational setting.

For a nurse checking vital signs on a bedside monitor in a dim, stressful ICU, good design means **large, high-contrast numbers and minimal steps**. HCI draws on computer science, software engineering, cognitive psychology, design and ergonomics.
