# Systems Concepts and the Systems Analyst

## Analysis and design

**Systems analysis and design** is the process of **examining a business situation with the intent of improving it** through better procedures and methods. **Systems analysis** is gathering and interpreting facts, diagnosing problems and recommending improvements: **analysis specifies what the system should do**. **Systems design** plans a new system to replace or complement an existing one: **design states how to accomplish the objectives**.

## What a system is

"System" comes from the Greek **systema**, an organised relationship among functioning units. A **system** is **a group of interrelated components working together toward a common goal by accepting inputs and producing outputs in an organised transformation process**. A class (instructor, students, textbooks) and a business (people, capital and materials aimed at profit) are both systems.

## Elements of a system

1. **Inputs and outputs**: inputs (materials, people, information) enter the system; **outputs** are its results and must meet users' expectations. Determining the output is the **first step** in specifying the input needed.
2. **Process**: the **actual transformation of input into output**.
3. **Control**: monitors and evaluates feedback to guide the system toward its goal, like the brain in the human system or management in a business.
4. **Feedback**: measures output against a standard and returns information for adjustment. **Positive** feedback reinforces performance; **negative** feedback gives the controller information for action.
5. **Environment**: the **"supra system"** outside the boundary that interacts with the system, such as vendors and competitors.
6. **Boundaries and interface**: the limits that define a system's scope. A bank teller system covers deposits and withdrawals but not mortgages.

## Types of systems

- **Closed** systems do not interact with their environment (mainly theoretical); **open** systems exchange inputs and outputs with it.
- **Deterministic** systems have certain, predictable outputs (a common entrance examination); **probabilistic** systems are predictable only by probability (stock investment); **random** systems are completely unpredictable.
- Others are **human, machine and human-machine**; **abstract and concrete**; **adaptive and non-adaptive**; and **simple and complex**.

A system must be designed to achieve a **predetermined objective**; its components must be **interrelated and interdependent**; and the **organisation's overall objectives take priority** over those of its subsystems.

## The systems analyst

A **systems analyst** studies an organisation's existing system and implements a new one for better performance, advising on suitable software, planning, developing and maintaining information systems. The **four attributes** of a systems analyst are:

1. **Business skills**: a clear view of the business environment.
2. **Information technology skills**: solid knowledge of current IT and of analysis and design.
3. **Human interaction skills**: understanding users' needs, with interviews a key part of the job.
4. **Managerial skills**: managing people, pressure and risk.
