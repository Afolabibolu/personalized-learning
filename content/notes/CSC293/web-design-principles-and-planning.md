# Web Design Principles and Planning

## What web design is

**Web design** is how content (usually hypertext or hypermedia) is **delivered to an end user through the Web** in a browser. Unlike print, the designer does not know the final size and shape of the page. Its basic aspects are:

- **Content**: relevant to the site and its target audience;
- **Usability**: user-friendly, with simple, reliable navigation;
- **Appearance**: one consistent, professional style; and
- **Structure** of the whole site.

Tables were once used for page layout, but **with CSS, table-based layout is regarded as outdated**.

## Static and dynamic pages

- **Static pages** do not change unless a person updates them; a plain HTML page is static.
- **Dynamic pages** adapt their content or appearance to the user's input or to changes such as the time or a database. They change on the **client side** with scripting such as **JavaScript**, or are built on the **server side** with **PHP, ASP, JSP or Perl**.

**Entertainment sites** are visited for fun, and their design may matter as much as the text. **Informational sites** deliver useful information fast, so **information matters more than design**.

## Components

Web design draws on graphic design, typography, human-computer interaction and **search engine optimisation**, and uses **markup languages** (HTML, XHTML, XML), **style sheets** (CSS), **client-side scripting** (JavaScript), **server-side scripting** (PHP, ASP) and **databases** (MySQL, PostgreSQL).

## Accessibility

Accessible pages follow the **WCAG** principles: use **semantic markup**, valid markup, **text equivalents for non-text content** such as images, and **links that make sense out of context** (avoid "Click here"). Assistive technologies such as **screen readers** depend on this.

## Planning

Planning defines the site's **architecture**, **purpose** (a purpose statement with short- and long-term goals and measurable objectives), **audience** (characteristics, information preferences, computer specifications and web experience) and **compatibility**: the only way to be sure a site works on a platform is to **test it there**.

## Responsive design and performance

**Responsive design** makes one site adapt to any screen, using **fluid grids**, **flexible images** and **CSS media queries**. **Mobile-first** design starts with the small screen and adds features for larger ones. Performance improves with smaller images, fewer requests, minified files and **caching**, which reuses stored copies but must be refreshed when content changes. A **single-page application (SPA)** loads one page and updates it with JavaScript, which feels fast but needs care for **search engine optimisation**. Sites that track users should collect only the data they need and respect privacy.
