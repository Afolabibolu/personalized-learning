# Data: Types, Sources, Quality and Measurement Scales

## Statistics

**Statistics** is the science of collecting, organising, analysing and interpreting data to make decisions. It is **domain neutral**: it applies to every field. It is used in two senses: statistics as **data** (scores, incomes, heights) and statistics as a **subject** (the methods). Broadly it is divided into **two** categories:

- **descriptive statistics** summarises data with tables, charts and measures such as the mean;
- **inferential statistics** draws conclusions about a population from a sample (estimation, hypothesis tests, prediction).

A **variable** is the characteristic or attribute that is measured. **Data** are the recorded values.

## Types of data

- **Qualitative (categorical)**: categories, such as gender or phone brand.
- **Quantitative (numerical)**: either **discrete**, as with counts such as the number of children, or **continuous**, which can take any value in a range, as with height or weight.

## Sources and collection

- **Primary** data are collected directly, through surveys, interviews, questionnaires, observation and experiments.
- **Secondary** data come from existing sources: unpublished records of organisations, published reports, journals and newspapers, and electronic databases and the internet.

Good data are **accessible, complete, accurate, interpretable, relevant, timely, secure and objective**.

## Measurement scales

Data come in **four levels**:

1. **Nominal**: names or labels with no order (gender, state of origin).
2. **Ordinal**: ordered categories where the differences are not meaningful (rankings, class of degree, grades).
3. **Interval**: equal differences but **no true zero** (temperature in °C, calendar years).
4. **Ratio**: equal differences and a **true zero**, so ratios are meaningful (weight, height, income).

CGPA is a numeric scale with equal steps but no meaningful zero of ability, so it is usually treated as **interval**. Higher scales allow more statistical operations: the mode for nominal data, the median for ordinal, and the mean for interval and ratio.
