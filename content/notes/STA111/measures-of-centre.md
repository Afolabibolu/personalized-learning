# Measures of Centre

## Measures of location

**Measures of location (central tendency, averages)** show where data are concentrated.

## The arithmetic mean

**x̄ = Σx/n**. For example, the ages 14, 15, 16, 16, 17, 17, 22, 22, 22, 22 have mean 183/10 = **18.3**.

- For frequency data, **x̄ = Σfx/Σf**.
- For **grouped** data, use the class marks for x.
- The **weighted mean** is Σwx/Σw, as in a CGPA.
- An unknown value can be found from the mean: if 2, 5, 3, w, 6 has mean 3.4, then w = 17 − 16 = 1.

The mean uses every value but is pulled by **extreme values (outliers)**.

## Geometric and harmonic means

- The **geometric mean** is **G = (x₁x₂...xₙ)^(1/n)**, or log G = (Σ log x)/n. It is used for rates of growth and ratios. For 2 and 8, G = 4.
- The **harmonic mean** is **H = n/Σ(1/x)**. It is used for average speeds over equal distances. For 2 and 8, H = 3.2.

For positive data, **A ≥ G ≥ H**: here 5 ≥ 4 ≥ 3.2.

## The median

The **median** is the middle value of ordered data: the **((n + 1)/2)th** value, or the average of the two middle values when n is even. For grouped data,

**Median = L + ((n/2 − F)/f) × c**,

where L is the lower boundary of the median class, F the cumulative frequency before it, f its frequency and c the class width. The median is **not affected by outliers**.

## The mode

The **mode** is the most frequent value. Data may be unimodal, bimodal or have no mode. For grouped data,

**Mode = L + (Δ₁/(Δ₁ + Δ₂)) × c**,

where Δ₁ and Δ₂ are the differences between the modal frequency and the frequencies of the classes before and after it. The mode is the only average for **nominal** data.

## Relationship

In a symmetric distribution, **mean = median = mode**. For moderate skewness, mean − mode ≈ 3(mean − median) (Pearson's empirical relation).
