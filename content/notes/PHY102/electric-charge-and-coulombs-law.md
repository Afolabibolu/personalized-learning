# Electric Charge and Coulomb's Law

## Charge

Charge comes in two kinds. **Like charges repel and unlike charges attract.** Ordinary matter is neutral: its electrons and protons balance.

- A body is **negatively charged** when it has **more electrons than protons**.
- It is **positively charged** when it is **deficient in electrons**.

Only electrons move in solids; protons stay in the nuclei.

Charge is **conserved** and **quantised** in multiples of e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

**Conductors** let charge move freely; **insulators** do not. When a positively charged object is **earthed**, **electrons flow from the ground to the object** and neutralise it.

## Coulomb's law

The force between two point charges q₁ and q₂ a distance r apart is

**F = k q₁q₂/r²**, where **k = 1/(4πε₀) ≈ 9 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻²** and ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C² N⁻¹ m⁻².

It is **directly proportional to the product of the charges** and **inversely proportional to the square of the distance**, and it acts along the line joining them.

**Examples**:

- Halving r multiplies F by 4: 96.48 N becomes **385.92 N**.
- Reducing each charge to a quarter and halving the distance gives F × (1/16) × 4 = **F/4**.
- In hydrogen (r = 5.3 × 10⁻¹¹ m), F = 9 × 10⁹ × (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹)²/(5.3 × 10⁻¹¹)² ≈ **8.2 × 10⁻⁸ N**.
- Two coins 1.5 m apart with equal charges and F = 2 N: q = r√(F/k) = **2.24 × 10⁻⁵ C**.

## Superposition

The net force on a charge is the **vector sum** of the forces from every other charge.

**Example**: q₁ = +6 μC and q₂ = +4 μC, 10 cm apart, with q_T = +2 μC midway (5 cm from each). The forces are 9 × 10⁹ × 12 × 10⁻¹²/0.0025 = 43.2 N away from q₁, and 28.8 N away from q₂. The net force is **14.4 N away from q₁**.

Compared with gravity, the electric force between particles is enormous: for an electron and a proton it is about 10³⁹ times larger.
