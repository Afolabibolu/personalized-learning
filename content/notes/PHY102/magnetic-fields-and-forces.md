# Magnetic Fields and Forces

## Magnetic fields

The source of magnetism is **electric charge in motion**. Static charges produce only electric fields; currents and magnets produce **magnetic fields B**. Field lines leave a magnet's north pole. The earth is a natural magnet (Gilbert, 1600), and its magnetic north pole lies near the geographic south.

## Force on a moving charge

**F = qv × B**, of magnitude **qvB sin θ**:

- it is proportional to q and v;
- it is greatest when v ⊥ B and zero when v ∥ B;
- it is **perpendicular to both v and B**, found by the **right-hand rule**, and reversed for negative charge.

The SI unit of B is the **tesla**: 1 T = 1 N/(A m) = 10⁴ gauss.

Because the force is always perpendicular to v, **a magnetic field does no work**. It changes the direction of motion but not the speed or the kinetic energy.

## Circular motion

In a uniform B, qvB = mv²/r, so **r = mv/(qB)**. The period **T = 2πm/(qB)** does not depend on speed or radius. This is the basis of the **cyclotron**, whose dees alternate at the cyclotron frequency f = qB/(2πm).

In **crossed E and B fields**, the forces balance when qE = qvB. So only particles with **v = E/B** pass undeflected: this is a **velocity selector**, used in the mass spectrometer.

## Force on a current

A wire of length L carrying current I in a field B feels **F = IL × B**, of magnitude **BIL sin θ** (Fleming's left-hand rule).

A rectangular coil of N turns and area A feels a **torque τ = NIAB sin θ**. This drives the **moving-coil galvanometer** and the d.c. motor.

## Fields of currents

From general knowledge (Biot–Savart and Ampère's laws):

- a long straight wire: **B = μ₀I/(2πr)**, in circles around it;
- the centre of a circular coil: μ₀NI/(2r);
- a long **solenoid**: **B = μ₀nI**, where n is the number of turns per metre;
- μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T m/A.

Parallel wires with currents in the **same direction attract**; opposite currents **repel**.
