# Electric Potential

## Potential

The **electric potential** V at a point is the work done per unit positive charge in bringing a test charge from infinity to that point: **V = W/q₀**, in **volts (J/C)**.

- Potential is a **scalar** quantity.
- It can be **positive or negative**: near a negative charge it is negative.
- The **potential difference** between two points is the work per unit charge in moving between them: **W = qΔV**.

The potential of a **point charge** is **V = kq/r**. For several charges, potentials add **algebraically**: no vectors are needed. This is a great simplification compared with fields.

## Potential energy

Two charges have **potential energy U = kq₁q₂/r**. It is positive for like charges and negative for unlike ones. For several charges, add U over every pair.

**Example**: +2 μC and +4 μC 10 cm apart have U = 9 × 10⁹ × 8 × 10⁻¹²/0.1 = **0.72 J**.

## Equipotentials and gradient

An **equipotential surface** has the same V everywhere. It is **perpendicular to the field lines**, and no work is done moving along it. Around a point charge the equipotentials are concentric spheres, and between parallel plates they are planes parallel to the plates.

The field is the **negative potential gradient**: **E = −dV/dx**, or in three dimensions E = −∇V. So E points from high to low potential, and in a uniform field E = V/d.

## Energy units

An **electronvolt** is the energy an electron gains through 1 V: **1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J**. An electron accelerated through V gains kinetic energy ½mv² = eV.

The potential of a charged conducting sphere is the same throughout: kQ/R at and inside the surface, and kQ/r outside it.
