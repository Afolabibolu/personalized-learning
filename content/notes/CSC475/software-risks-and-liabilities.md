# Software Issues: Risks and Liabilities

## Software and its buyers

**Software** is a set of programs made of instructions telling the computer what to do, built into permanent memory or loaded into memory. Issues arise between **developers** and **buyers** over claims, user expectations and the legal results of an unhealthy relationship.

## Standards, verification and reliability

- **Standards:** developers must show buyers their products are of high quality, but buyers have little leverage because there is no single universal standard.
- **Verification and validation (V&V):** static formal techniques such as **proof of correctness** and dynamic techniques such as **testing** show that the code is consistent with its specification.
- **Reliability:** unlike hardware, software reliability cannot be measured by wear and tear or by production quantities, and a program with fewer errors is not necessarily more reliable. No system can be certified error-free.

## Security, safety and quality

- **Software security** is threatened as more vital information moves onto computers, by software passing through many forms during development and by the **human element**.
- **Safety** matters in critical systems such as space exploration, aircraft guidance and life support; software that is safe in one environment may be unsafe in another.
- **Quality** has come to the fore with the global software market; **quality of service (QoS)** means consistent, predictable delivery that satisfies the customer's requirements.

## Causes of software failure

Failures come from human error, the nature of software and its environment.

**Human factors:**

1. Memory lapses and attentional failures.
2. A **rush to finish** under management pressure.
3. **Overconfidence** and nonstandard or untested algorithms.
4. **Malice**, such as Trojan horses.
5. **Complacency** of experienced developers.

**The nature of software:**

1. **Complexity:** billions of possible outcomes.
2. **Difficult testing:** no complete set of tests can exist.
3. **Ease of programming**, which draws people without proper training.
4. **Misunderstood design specifications.**

## Hazards and risk

A **hazard** is a state of a system that, together with other conditions in its environment, will inevitably lead to an accident. **Risk** is a hazard level together with the likelihood of an accident and the severity of its consequences: the potential for harm or loss. Risk is **voluntary** (activities we choose) or **involuntary** (such as lightning or fire). Software risks include personnel shortfalls, unrealistic schedules and budgets, and developing the wrong functions.

## Risk assessment and management

**Risk management** estimates the impact of risk by measuring a system's assets and vulnerabilities, assessing threats and monitoring security. During design it documents the probability and consequences of hazards, ranks risks, and plans, implements and monitors mitigation strategies. In use it has three phases:

- **Assessment:** identifying vulnerabilities by question and answer, qualitative assessment or calculation.
- **Planning:** outlining policies for security management.
- **Implementation:** matching the system's security needs with the available tools.
