# Morality and the Law

## Morality

**Morality** is a set of rules for right conduct, a system used to modify and regulate behaviour by which we judge acts right or wrong, good or bad. It builds virtues such as love for others, compassion and a desire for justice. It can be defined **descriptively** (the code of conduct a society actually holds), **normatively** (an ideal code all rational people would observe, as in "murder is immoral") or as a synonym of **ethics**. Morality is territorial, cultural and time sensitive.

## Moral decision making

A good moral decision considers **all the facts** and the interests of all parties, and the **moral principles** involved, so it needs reasoning and impartiality. These are ensured by rational intuition of moral principles, reason to reach the highest good, distinguishing **primary** (general) from **secondary** (specific) principles, and calculating the **consequences** of actions.

## Moral codes

**Moral codes** are a group's rules or norms of proper behaviour. Sagan listed near-universal rules:

- **Golden Rule:** do unto others as you would have them do unto you (found in Buddhist, Christian, Confucian, Islamic, Jewish and other traditions).
- **Silver Rule:** do not do unto others what you would not have them do unto you.
- **Bronze Rule:** repay kindness with kindness.
- **Iron Rule:** do unto others as you like, before they do it unto you (it can create dictatorships).
- **Tin Rule:** pay homage to those above you and intimidate those below you (the bully rule).
- **Nepotism Rule:** give precedence to close relatives (it legitimizes corruption).

Group codes include the Native American, Jewish and Christian Ten Commandments.

## Moral standards, guilt and conscience

A **moral standard** is the norm against which actions are judged. **Moral guilt** comes from judging that one has not lived up to the standards; self-forgiveness limits its build-up. **Conscience**, from the Latin *conscientia* ("knowing with"), is the inner voice that judges our actions. Religion, unlike morality, draws on divine will and judgment.

## Law

**Law** is a rule of conduct recognized by custom or decreed by formal enactment.

- **Natural law** is unwritten, universal and discoverable by reason.
- **Conventional law** is made by people, for example in legislatures; it is **declarative** (restating natural law, such as forbidding murder) or **determinative** (such as contracts, taxes and traffic). Examples are the Roman *jus gentium* and English common law.

Laws protect **life, liberty and property**. The **penal code** prescribes punishment, which is **retributive**, **corrective** and **deterrent**.

## Morality versus law

Laws are made by authorities, morality by all members of society over time. Laws are enforced by courts and police, morality is self-enforced. Law resolves **interpersonal** conflicts, morality **intrapersonal** ones, and morality judges intentions and character. **Etiquette** is a code of correct social behaviour, usually unwritten and unenforced.
