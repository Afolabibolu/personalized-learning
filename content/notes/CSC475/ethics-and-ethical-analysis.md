# Ethics and Ethical Analysis

## Meaning of ethics

**Ethics** is the study of right and wrong in human conduct: a theoretical examination of morality. Robert C. Solomon defined it as a set of theories of value, virtue or right action. Ethics distinguishes right from wrong and explains **why** a judgment of an action is justified. It interprets human conduct through argument, a mixture of **induction** and **deduction**, based on **ethical theories**, and codes of ethics are drawn up from these theories.

## Ethical theories

**Consequentialism** judges actions by their results: a desirable result means a good action.

- **Egoism:** an action is good if it maximizes the individual's own happiness. **Ethical egoism** says how people ought to behave; **psychological egoism** describes how they actually behave.
- **Utilitarianism:** an action is good if it benefits the maximum number of people. **Act utilitarianism** weighs the consequences of each action; **rule utilitarianism** follows rules that bring the greatest happiness to the greatest number.
- **Altruism:** an action is right if its consequences are favourable to all except the actor.

Other theories:

- **Deontology** ignores consequences and judges the **will** behind an action: an act is good if the person had a good reason, and it carries a **duty**.
- **Human nature:** people have capabilities for happiness, and actions are judged by how well they measure up to them.
- **Relativism** denies universal moral norms: right and wrong are relative to society, culture or the individual, and not fixed in time.
- **Hedonism:** pleasure is the only good and the highest end of life; it has psychological and ethical forms.
- **Emotivism:** ethical statements are neither true nor false; they only express how someone feels.

## Ethical reasoning and decision making

**Reasoning** is the cognitive process of generating or affirming a proposition; **ethical reasoning** integrates ethical principles into that process, because reasoning by intuition can lead to wrong decisions. A good **framework for ethical decision making** includes:

- recognizing the ethical conflicts by evaluating all ethical dimensions of the problem;
- understanding the problem and its facts;
- knowing the parties involved;
- being aware of the alternatives;
- demonstrating knowledge of ethical practices;
- understanding how the decision will be implemented, who will be affected and what the impact will be.

Using these elements lessens the number of conflicts and the severity of their impact.

## Making and evaluating ethical arguments

Ethical reasoning works in layers that are revised as facts come in. It must tell the decision maker whether the problem really is ethical, whether more facts would help, which ethical theories, principles and values fit the alternatives, and how strong and valid the chosen theory is. The decision is then **evaluated**: reasons are weak if they rest on **false or unsupported assumptions**, and a reasoning is **valid** if its premises are true, making its conclusion very probably true.
