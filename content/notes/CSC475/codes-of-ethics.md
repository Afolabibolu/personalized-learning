# Codes of Ethics

## What codes of ethics are

**Codes of ethics** are the rules, guidelines, canons and advisories followed by members of a professional body, such as the **ACM** and the **IEEE**. Ethics is governed through:

1. **Principles**, which act as guidelines or bases for documents.
2. **Public policies**, setting acceptable behaviour, norms and practices.
3. **Codes of conduct**, which include ethical principles.
4. **Legal instruments**, which enforce good conduct through the courts.

Codes of ethics are still used mainly by professions and high-visibility institutions, but their use is spreading.

## The ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct

On 16 October 1992, the ACM Executive Council adopted a revised Code of Ethics. Its imperatives are expressed generally, because the ethical principles of computer ethics come from more general ethical principles, and it is supplemented by guidelines that explain them.

### General moral imperatives

As an ACM member I will:

1. Contribute to society and human well-being.
2. Avoid harm to others.
3. Be honest and trustworthy.
4. Be fair and take action not to discriminate.
5. Honor property rights including copyrights and patents.
6. Give proper credit for intellectual property.
7. Respect the privacy of others.
8. Honor confidentiality.

### More specific professional responsibilities

As an ACM computing professional I will:

1. Strive to achieve the highest quality, effectiveness and dignity in the process and products of professional work.
2. Acquire and maintain professional competence.
3. Know and respect existing laws pertaining to professional work.
4. Accept and provide appropriate professional review.
5. Give comprehensive and thorough evaluations of computer systems and their impacts, including possible risks.
6. Honor contracts, agreements and assigned responsibilities.
7. Improve public understanding of computing and its consequences.
8. Access computing and communication resources only when authorized to do so.

### Organizational leadership imperatives

As an ACM member and organizational leader I will:

1. Articulate the social responsibilities of members of an organizational unit and encourage their acceptance.
2. Manage personnel and resources to build information systems that enhance the quality of working life.
3. Acknowledge and support proper and authorized uses of an organization's computing resources.
4. Ensure that users' needs are clearly articulated during the assessment and design of requirements.
5. Articulate and support policies that protect the dignity of users.
6. Create opportunities for members to learn the principles and limitations of computer systems.

## Why the imperatives matter

"Contribute to society and human well-being", for example, affirms an obligation to protect **fundamental human rights** and respect the **diversity of all cultures**. Members are expected to comply with the code, and the guidelines may change while the code stays stable.
