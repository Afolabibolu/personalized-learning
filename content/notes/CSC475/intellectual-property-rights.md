# Intellectual Property Rights and Computer Technology

## Intellectual property

**Intellectual property (IP)** describes things such as ideas, inventions, technologies, artworks, music and literature to which one can claim ownership; **intellectual property rights** are the legal rights that protect them. Computer **products** (keyboard, CPU, monitor) have tangible form; **services** (repairs, network installation) do not; **software** passes through a logic map, source code, object code, memory-based executable code and microcode.

## Copyright

**Copyright** is a legal right accorded to the creator of an **expression**: literary, dramatic, musical, artistic and audiovisual works and sound recordings. Ideas, facts, names, titles, calendars and blank forms are excluded. A copyright notice has the © symbol, the word "copyright", the year and the owner's name. Protection requires **fixation** (a tangible form, such as binary code) and **originality**. When copyright expires, the work enters the **public domain**.

## Patents

A **patent** protects an **invention or discovery**. To be patentable it must be a new and useful process, manufacture, machine or composition of matter, and meet four conditions:

1. **Utility:** a useful purpose that is not dangerous, illegal or immoral.
2. **Novelty:** not used, known or published before.
3. **Non-obviousness** to a person of ordinary skill.
4. **Disclosure** of how it works.

The book gives the term of protection as **17 years**; patent law protects the process of carrying out an idea, not the idea itself.

## Trade secrets

A **trade secret** is information giving a business a competitive advantage, such as a formula or design. It is characterized by how widely it is known outside the business, the measures taken to keep it secret, its value to owner and competitor, the effort spent developing it, and how hard it is to duplicate.

## Trademarks

A **trademark** is a label (word, name, picture or symbol) identifying a product or service. The term includes **service marks**, **certification marks** and **collective marks**. Marks are **arbitrary**, **suggestive**, **descriptive** or **general**. A registered mark must be in good taste, not a symbol of a recognized country, and not use people's likenesses without consent.

## Crimes and infringement

An **intellectual property crime** is infringement, counterfeiting or piracy of products and services for profit. Infringement is **direct** (making, using, selling or copying a protected item), **inducement** (intentionally supporting infringement) or **contributory** (taking part in it). Copyright, patent and trademark infringement are all hard to prove; trademark infringement requires showing that the public was likely to be confused. **Software piracy** is countered by protecting software under copyright, patent and trademark laws.

**Identity theft**, misrepresenting oneself as another person to obtain goods and services, uses techniques such as pretext calls, telemarketing scams, fake IDs and searching trash; it is prevented by shredding documents, checking credit reports and reporting incidents.
