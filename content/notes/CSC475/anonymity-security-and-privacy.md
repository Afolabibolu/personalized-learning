# Anonymity, Security, Privacy and Civil Liberties

## Anonymity

**Anonymity** is being nameless, having no identity. Its forms are **pseudo-identity** (a pseudonym, code or number, as in witness protection), **untraceable identity** and anonymity with a **pseudo-address** (anonymous remailers). On the Internet it comes through **anonymous servers**, either full anonymity or pseudonymous servers, and **anonymous users** in chat rooms and social networks. Neither is 100% anonymous.

**Advantages:** it protects **whistle-blowers**, supports national security, lets information come out where people fear reprisal, and protects some relationships. **Disadvantages:** criminals and embezzlers hide behind it, and disputes cannot be resolved without identities.

## Security

**Security** prevents unauthorized access, use, alteration, theft or physical damage. It has three elements:

- **Confidentiality:** preventing unauthorized disclosure.
- **Integrity:** preventing unauthorized modification.
- **Availability:** preventing information being withheld from those who need it.

**Physical security** relies on **deterrence**, **prevention**, **detection** and **response**, with physical barriers (fences, CCTV, sensors) and electronic access controls.

## Information security

- **Passwords:** never publicize one, never write it down, never choose one that is easy to guess, and never keep it for long.
- **Firewalls** isolate sensitive parts of a system: **packet filters**, **proxy servers** and **stateful inspection** (which combines both).
- **Encryption** protects channels from sniffers. **Symmetric** encryption uses one shared key, whose distribution is a problem; **asymmetric (public-key)** encryption uses a public and a private key.
- **Authentication** verifies users by username, password and physical methods; a **digital signature** signs a document with a hash (message digest) and proves who signed it.

## Privacy

**Privacy** has four elements: **solitude** (the right to be alone), **anonymity** (no public identity), **intimacy** (not to be monitored) and **reserve** (control of one's personal information). Its types are **personal**, **informational** (personal, financial, medical and Internet information) and **institutional** privacy. Privacy matters because it guards **personal identity**, preserves **autonomy** and makes **social relationships** possible.

Threats include **information gathering** and databases, Internet crawlers, **intrusion**, **misuse** and **interception** of information, and **information matching**.

## Civil liberties

The most accepted civil liberties are criminal justice (police powers, personal liberty, a fair trial), basic freedoms (speech, assembly, association, movement, no discrimination), freedom of information, and communications and privacy. Privacy is protected by **technical** safeguards and user education, such as not revealing personal information, managing cookies, keeping a clean email address and remembering that you may be monitored at work.
