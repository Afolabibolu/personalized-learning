# Leadership, Motivation and Communication

## Leadership

**Leadership** is the art or process of influencing people so that they strive willingly and enthusiastically towards group goals. It is a process, not a position. Leadership and management are distinct but complementary, so all managers should also be good leaders. Leadership raises morale, creates teamwork, keeps the group united and maintains discipline.

## Approaches to leadership

**Trait approach:** effective leaders were thought to share traits such as intelligence and drive (the "Great Man" theory). It failed because many non-leaders have the traits and research could not agree on them.

**Behavioural approach (leadership styles):**

- **Autocratic:** all authority with the leader and one-way communication. Decisions are quick, but morale suffers and it breeds a "yes" mentality.
- **Democratic (participative):** members contribute to decisions, with two-way communication.
- **Laissez-faire:** the leader exercises very little control, which can leave the group without cohesion.

**Likert's System 4** (participative) management was the most successful, and **Blake and Mouton's managerial grid** rates styles from 1,1 to 9,9.

**Situational (contingency) approach:**

- **Tannenbaum and Schmidt** saw a continuum of styles.
- **Fiedler** said the right style depends on **position power**, **task structure** and **leader-member relations**.

## Motivation

**Motivation** is the drive that makes people act towards goals. **Intrinsic** motivators last longer than **extrinsic** ones.

**Content theories:**

- **Maslow's hierarchy of needs:** physiological, safety, social, esteem and self-actualization. When a lower need is satisfied, the next becomes the motivator.
- **Herzberg's two-factor theory:**
  - **hygiene factors** (pay, conditions, policy) prevent dissatisfaction but do not motivate;
  - **motivators** (achievement, recognition, responsibility, growth) motivate.

  This leads to **job enrichment**.

**Process theories:**

- **Vroom's expectancy theory (1964):** motivation depends on expecting that effort leads to performance, and that performance leads to valued rewards.
- **Adams' equity theory:** people compare their output-to-input ratio with others, and act to restore equity when they feel under- or over-rewarded.

## Communication

**Communication** is the transfer and understanding of meaning between people, through a sender, a message, a channel, a receiver and feedback.

- Channels run **downward**, **upward** and **horizontally**, plus the informal **grapevine**.
- Barriers include noise, poor listening, jargon, status differences and information overload.

## Groups and change

**Groups** are **formal** (set up by the organization) or **informal** (formed by members). Managing **change** means overcoming resistance through participation and communication. **Lewin's** model unfreezes the old way, changes, then refreezes the new way.
