# Organizing and Staffing

## Organizing

**Organizing** is the second function of management: designing and maintaining a system of roles, creating a structure that lets people work together towards objectives. It avoids confusion of roles, clarifies responsibilities and sets clear channels of communication.

An **organization** is the group of people working together; its **organizational structure** is the formal arrangement of jobs, departments and reporting lines, shown on an **organizational chart**. The steps of organizational design are:

1. reflect on the plans and objectives;
2. divide the major tasks into sub-tasks;
3. allocate resources;
4. evaluate the results.

**Principles of organizing:**

- **division of work** and specialization;
- **departmentation** (grouping related activities);
- **coordination**;
- **chain of command** (reporting lines);
- **unity of command** (one manager per subordinate);
- **span of control** (subordinates per manager).

## Authority, power, responsibility and accountability

- **Authority** is the right to command.
- **Power** is the ability to do so, from rewards, punishment, expertise or position.
- **Responsibility** is the obligation to perform; it cannot be delegated.
- **Accountability** is answering to a superior for the authority used.

Authority and power flow down, accountability flows up, and responsibility stays with the individual. The four must be balanced: too much power leads to abuse, too little authority to ineffectiveness. **Delegation** gives a subordinate authority to do part of the supervisor's job, but planning, organizing, leading and control cannot be delegated.

## Organizational designs

- **Functional:** groups specialists such as marketing, HR, finance and IT; the most common design.
- **Product:** a manager for each product line.
- **Geographical:** a manager for each region.
- **Customer:** units for groups of customers, as in banks.

**Line** functions contribute directly to the organization's goals, while **staff** functions advise and support them. **Centralization** keeps authority at the top; large organizations tend to decentralize. An effective **span of control** depends on the rate of change, communication methods, subordinates' skills and the work involved.

## Staffing

**Staffing** fills positions with the right people.

1. **Job analysis** produces a **job description** (title, duties, reporting line, qualifications and conditions).
2. **Recruitment** attracts applicants through advertisements, referrals, agencies or internal promotion.
3. **Selection** screens applications and uses tests and interviews to choose the best candidate.
4. **Induction and training** follow.
5. **Performance appraisal** measures results.
6. **Rewards and promotion** should be fair and based on merit and performance, with salary, bonuses and recognition.

Employing relatives without regard to merit (**nepotism**) can bring loyalty, but it often lowers competence and morale.
