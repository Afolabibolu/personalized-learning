# Planning and Objectives

## Meaning of planning

**Planning** is deciding what objectives to accomplish, the actions to take to achieve them, the organizational positions assigned, and who is responsible. It **precedes all other managerial functions** and is the "star or compass" that directs the enterprise. It is pervasive (done by all managers at all levels), and plans must be **efficient**, judged by their contribution to objectives at the lowest cost, including intangible costs such as low morale.

## Types of plans

- **Strategic plans:** broad plans by top managers on the firm's direction and what business it is in.
- **Tactical plans:** moderate scope; how to implement strategic plans.
- **Operational plans:** the narrowest focus. They include **standing plans** (policies, rules and procedures for recurring situations) and **single-use plans** (for unique situations, such as a project).

By time, plans are **long-range** (over five years), **intermediate** (under five years) and **short-range** (one year or less).

Planning happens at three levels: **corporate** (mission, overall strategy), **business** (how a division competes) and **functional** (departments such as marketing and R&D).

**Purpose** is the role society gives all organizations of a type, **mission** is the unique aim that sets one apart, **objectives** are measurable targets, and **strategy** is the broad programme for achieving them.

## The planning process

1. Be aware of opportunities.
2. Establish objectives.
3. Develop **planning premises** (assumptions about the environment).
4. Determine alternative courses of action.
5. Evaluate them against risk, profit, cost and technology.
6. Select a course of action.
7. Formulate derivative plans.
8. Quantify plans by **budgeting**.

## Management by objectives

**MBO** (Peter Drucker, 1954) integrates individual and organizational goals: managers and subordinates agree goals, set checkpoints and evaluate results.

- **Benefits:** better managing, clearer roles, personal commitment, effective controls and improved communication.
- **Weaknesses:** failure to teach its philosophy, no guidelines for goal-setters, difficulty setting goals, and inflexibility.

## Barriers to planning

Plans fail through:

- lack of commitment;
- unclear objectives;
- ignoring premises;
- over-reliance on experience;
- lack of top support;
- poor delegation and control;
- resistance to change;
- time and cost.

They are avoided by starting at the top, participation, communication, integration of plans and **contingency planning**.

## SWOT and PEST

A **SWOT** analysis lists a firm's internal **Strengths and Weaknesses** and external **Opportunities and Threats**. **PEST** examines the **Political, Economic, Social and Technological** forces in its macro environment. Both help a new IT company choose strategy and location.
