# Computer Science as a Discipline

## What computer scientists do

Computer scientists **create and manage tools that support and enhance the use of the modern computer** (computation tools). Before a tool is built they create **models** of it, mostly mathematical and built on **discrete structures** such as sets, lists and orderings. From these they create **theories**, bodies of knowledge that make models possible, and tools are built on the theories.

Computer science is **the systematic study of algorithmic processes that describe and transform information**: their theory, analysis, design, efficiency, implementation and application. Its underlying question is **what can be automated?**

## Solving problems computationally

1. **Abstraction**: build a clean model of the real-world problem that removes complex details. Driving a car is an abstract view: the engine's workings are hidden.
2. **Automation**: use the model to solve problems through **algorithms** that act on real data.
3. **Implementation**: write the algorithms as **code** the computer can run, which proves the solution works.

## The discipline

The first computer science degree was the **Cambridge Diploma (1953)**. The first US department was at **Purdue University (1962)**, and the **University of Ibadan** department dates from **1974**.

The discipline has two areas:

- **Systems**: algorithms and data structures, programming languages and compilers, architecture, operating systems, software engineering, HCI and networks.
- **Applications**: numerical computation, databases, AI and robotics, graphics, bioinformatics, the Web and games.

It draws on three traditions:

- **Mathematics** gives it theory.
- **Engineering** gives it creativity and design.
- **Experimental science** gives it its science.

A computer is organised in **layers**: information, hardware, programming, operating system, application and communication.

## Goals for tools

Tools are built to meet six goals:

- **Correctness**: the tool meets a clear, unambiguous specification.
- **Efficiency**: judicious use of time and space.
- **Security**: resistance to malicious attack. **Encryption** stops interception.
- **User-friendliness**: ease of use, which depends on context.
- **Fault-tolerance**: coping with faults, measured by **MTBF** (mean time between failures).
- **Intelligence**: mimicking human reasoning, language, learning and game playing. IBM's **Deep Blue** beat Garry Kasparov in **1997**.

A good computer scientist has a passion for elegant solutions, mathematical rigour, creativity with abstractions, attention to detail and the habit of reusing known solutions. **Computer engineering** assembles hardware and software for the best cost-performance, while computer science cares about feasibility and efficiency less tied to current technology. The **digital divide** is the gap between those who have access to computer technology and those who do not; in Nigeria it shows in rural communities and schools.
