# Computer Categories and Hardware

## Categories

A **special-purpose** computer runs the same stored instructions for one function (microwave ovens, washing machines, medical equipment). A **general-purpose** computer solves many kinds of problems. It comes as:

- **Microcomputers**: personal computers. They include laptops (notebooks), desktops and **workstations**, the largest microcomputers, used in science and engineering.
- **Minicomputers**: more powerful than micros and cheaper than mainframes, often used as **servers**.
- **Mainframes**: massive memory and very fast processing for large business, scientific or military work.
- **Supercomputers**: for extremely rapid, complex calculation, such as weather prediction, aircraft design and research.

## Hardware

A computer **accepts input, stores it, processes it and outputs** results. **Hardware** is the physical part; **software** is the instructions. The **von Neumann architecture** has three essential parts:

1. **input/output devices**;
2. **memory**, holding data *and* programs;
3. the **central processing unit (CPU)**.

The **motherboard** (system board) holds the processor, memory and adapter cards.

The **CPU** has a **control unit**, which directs and coordinates operations by reading the stored program one instruction at a time, and an **arithmetic logic unit (ALU)**. The ALU does arithmetic and logic, and decides **greater than, less than and equal to**. Each **machine cycle** is **fetch, decode, execute, store**.

**Buses** are the circuitry paths for data and signals:

- the **data bus** passes information both ways (**bi-directional**);
- the **address bus** carries the location in primary storage;
- the **control bus** says whether to read or write.

## Memory and storage

**Primary storage (main memory)** holds the running program, the operating system and the data in use.

- **RAM** is short-term storage and is **volatile**. **SRAM** uses flip-flops and is faster; **DRAM** is the most common and must be re-energised constantly.
- **ROM** is **non-volatile** and read-only. **PROM** is a blank ROM chip that can be written once; **EEPROM** can be erased electrically.

Sizes run in KB, MB, GB and TB.

**Secondary storage** is non-volatile and peripheral:

- **Magnetic disks** (floppy and hard) are **direct access** devices, divided into tracks and sectors of 512 bytes.
- **Optical discs**: CD-ROM holds 650 MB to 1 GB, DVD 4.7 to 17 GB, Blu-ray up to 27 GB. CD-R can be written once and CD-RW many times.
- **Magnetic tape** is cheap but **sequential** and slow, so it is used for backup.

**Input devices** include the keyboard, mouse, trackball, touch screen and light pen. **Source data automation** captures data where it is created, using OCR, OMR, bar codes and **MICR**, the magnetic ink used for **cheques in banking**. **Output devices** include monitors and printers, both impact (dot matrix) and non-impact (laser, inkjet).
