# HCI, Interactive Systems and Usability

## What the topic is

Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) is the study of how people interact with computers. It has three entities: the **user** (an individual or a group), the **computer** (anything from a PC to large systems, mobile devices and websites) and the **interaction**, sometimes called the interface: the point where the two meet. The goal of HCI is to produce usable and safe systems by putting people first, so that systems match users' needs rather than the other way round.

## Subtopics

**Interactive systems: past, present and future.** Before 1980 interactive design was not common: little attention was paid to users, and people used punched cards with long waits. Today the user is in control, and user and system exchange information frequently and dynamically. A good interactive system lets the user easily figure out how to operate it and easily evaluate the result of their actions.

**Good and poor design.** The core of interaction design is a usable product. We learn to recognise one by comparing good and poor designs and identifying the strengths and weaknesses of existing products.

**Cultural factors and digital inclusion.** Technology adoption in Africa is affected by language (most applications are in English, which only about 30% of Nigerians speak), cultural beliefs (myths about some technologies) and behavioural patterns: users must be able to identify with the app.

**Usability goals.** A usable system is effective (does what it should), efficient (no unnecessarily long or repeated steps; caching or auto-save help), safe, learnable, memorable and enjoyable. To reduce memory load: describe the required input format, show examples of valid input and provide default inputs.

**UX and design principles.** "You can't design a user experience, you rather design for a user experience": a slim phone that fits the hand supports a good experience; a big, heavy one gives a poor one. Design principles include feedback, constraints, visibility, consistency and affordance.

**Interaction design as a process.** It is goal-directed, creative and about balancing trade-offs, with four basic activities: establishing requirements, designing alternatives, prototyping and evaluating.

## Key terms

- **Usability:** easy to learn, remember, use effectively, efficiently and safely, and enjoyable.
- **Memorability:** how easily usage is remembered after infrequent use.
- **Disciplines contributing to HCI:** psychology, sociology, information systems, product design, computer science.

## Exam-style point

Past papers ask how banks can keep online banking secure while easing the memory load. Use the memorability goal: state the required input format, show valid examples and provide sensible defaults, so security steps do not depend on recall alone.
