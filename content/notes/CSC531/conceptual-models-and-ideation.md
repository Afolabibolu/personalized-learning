# Conceptual Models, Interaction Types and Ideation

## What the topic is

Once the problem space is understood, the design space can be conceptualised. A **conceptual model** is a high-level description of how the system works and what it does. It can be written as text or drawn as a diagram, and it becomes a blueprint once the whole design team agrees on it. Its key benefit is that it lets designers "straighten out their thinking before they start laying out their widgets". Ideation is how the team generates and selects the ideas behind it.

## Subtopics

**Interface metaphors.** Metaphors give familiar entities that help people understand the conceptual model and know what to do at an interface. Interaction metaphors take their names from everyday, non-digital objects and mimic real-life concepts and symbols people already know.

**Interaction types.** The ways a person interacts with a product:
- **Instructing:** telling the system what to do, by pressing buttons or typing commands (print a file, set a reminder).
- **Conversing:** the system acts as a dialogue partner, as in advisory systems, help facilities, chatbots and robots.
- **Manipulating:** opening, holding, closing and placing objects in a virtual or physical space (MS Word, image editors).
- **Exploring:** moving through virtual or physical environments, such as a 3D building.
- **Responding:** system-initiated, where the system alerts the user to something relevant, like a fitness tracker reporting steps walked.

**Representing a conceptual model.** Diagrammatic forms include ER diagrams, flowcharts and use cases. Storyboards come from film and animation: they give a "script" of the important interactions. Avoid unnecessary detail, keep text short, show people experiencing the design and their reactions, and give end-users fewer frames (typically 4 to 6).

**Ideation.** It comes after problem definition and needs a skilled facilitator and rules: a time limit, no bad ideas, no distractions. Team members build on each other's ideas. Techniques:
- **Brainstorming:** group discussion that builds on ideas.
- **Brainwriting:** ideas written on cards and passed round for others to build on.
- **Brainwalking:** ideas on flip charts around the wall, with people walking round to add.
- **Brain dumping:** one idea per sticky note, then grouped on a board.

The best ideas are chosen with dot voting, bingo selection or affinity diagrams. The agreed solution need not be the single "best idea". Useful tools are flip charts, sticky notes, markers and Microsoft Visio.

## Key terms

- **Conceptual model:** a high-level, agreed description of the system's operation.
- **Storyboard:** a sequence of frames showing the key interactions.

## Exam-style point

"A hotel robot takes room-service orders by phone: voice recognition or phone buttons?" Voice is conversing: natural, but error-prone with accents and noise. Buttons are instructing: reliable, but slower and more limited.
