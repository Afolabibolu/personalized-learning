# Cognitive Psychology and Perception

## What the topic is

Cognitive psychology is the science of how we think: the branch of psychology concerned with how our minds process information from the senses. It matters in HCI because designing for humans requires understanding how they process information, perceive the world, focus attention and use memory, so that systems are sympathetic to users' capabilities and limitations.

## Subtopics

**Cognition and perception.** Cognition covers thinking, remembering, learning, decision making, reading and writing. Experiential cognition is intuitive and effortless (eating). Reflective cognition requires mental effort (writing). Perception is how information is acquired from the environment through the sense organs.

**The senses.** The five major senses are sight, hearing, touch, taste and smell. Vision is the most dominant in perception, then hearing and touch.

**Visual perception: two theories.** Constructivism says we do not perceive the world directly: we process any image before it is consciously recognised, and context helps. The same "-" symbol can mean different things in different interface contexts, and the same shape can be read as "B" or "13". The ecological theory says information is extracted directly from the light captured by the eye, using world knowledge built up since childhood. That is why we recognise objects even when parts are hidden.

**Gestalt principles.** Gestalt psychology looks at the mind and behaviour as a whole. Its principles describe how we group elements, recognise patterns and simplify images; designers use them to organise interfaces. Four lines in a square are seen as a "square", not four lines. The variations:
- **Common region:** items within a boundary are perceived as a group.
- **Figure-ground:** we separate an object (figure) from its surroundings (ground).
- **Continuity:** elements on a line or curve seem more related.
- **Closure:** the brain fills in missing parts to see a complete pattern, which is common in logos.
- **Proximity:** elements close together are seen as belonging together, as on menus.
- **Similarity** and **focal point** complete the list.

**Cognitive processes.** These are attention, perception, memory, learning, reading, speaking and listening, and problem solving, planning, reasoning and decision making. Attention lets us select what to focus on; a clear goal helps, and how information is presented affects how easily it is found. Information should be presented so it is readily perceived as intended.

## Key terms

- **Constructivist vs ecological:** context-driven processing vs direct pickup with world knowledge.
- **Gestalt:** perceiving a whole that is more than its parts.

## Exam-style point

"What is a gestalt? Give an example." A gestalt is the whole we perceive from separate parts: four lines arranged in a square are seen as one square. Proximity is a design example, where related website content is grouped with empty space around it.
