# Africa and the Early Humans

## Reconstructing the past

The efforts to reconstruct the human past fall into two: **prehistory**, the period **before written records**, and **history**, which covers the period of written records. Prehistory is known from the remains early humans left, mainly **tools, artifacts and animal bones** found by **archaeological excavation**. When prehistory ends and history begins varies greatly between places. Prehistoric cultures are named in stages, oldest first:

1. **Paleolithic** (Old Stone Age): chipped stone implements.
2. **Mesolithic** (Middle Stone Age): small, finely trimmed, flat and thin stone implements.
3. **Neolithic** (New Stone Age): **polished stone** implements, food production and the domestication of animals.
4. **Bronze Age**: bronze implements.
5. **Iron Age**: iron implements.

These names are cultural stages rather than fixed periods of time.

## The cradle of humankind

The place of origin of humans is **still uncertain**, but the old theory of **Central Asia** as the cradle of mankind rested on false premises. The idea that humans evolved in Africa goes back to **Charles Darwin**, whose **The Descent of Man** (1871) argued that Africa was probably the cradle because humans' closest relatives, chimpanzees and gorillas, live there. Discoveries by Raymond Dart and **L.S.B. Leakey** support an evolutionary line:

- **Australopithecus** (the Southern Ape), believed to have flourished in East Africa about four million years ago; rich remains come from **Olduvai Gorge** in Tanzania and Hadar in Ethiopia. Its two species were Africanus (slender, omnivorous) and Robustus (gorilla-sized, mainly vegetarian). It could plan complex ventures and travel deliberately over distances.
- **Homo habilis** (handy man), from about 2 million years ago, made the earliest intentionally shaped stone tools (chopper and chopping tools).
- **Homo erectus** (upright walking man) used fire, hunted in coordinated bands that presumed language, and spread to North Africa, Asia and Europe. It was the **predecessor of Homo sapiens**.
- **Homo sapiens** (consciously thinking human), from 200,000 to 100,000 years ago, had a large brain, used knives, spears, bows and arrows, built shelters and spoke languages.

## Hunters and gatherers

Throughout the Paleolithic, people **foraged**: men hunted, while women and children gathered plants, roots, nuts and fruits, which made up as much as three quarters of the diet. Bones became tools and skins became clothing and shelter. Foraging kept communities small and mobile.
