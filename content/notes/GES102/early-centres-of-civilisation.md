# Early Centres of Civilisation and Roman Africa

## Egypt

**Egypt** was the earliest centre of African civilisation. By 5,000 BC immigrants had introduced food production, and the **River Nile** gave silt for farming, a line of communication and contact with southern neighbours for gold, wood and ivory. Egypt was ruled by a **divine king, the Pharaoh**, with a bureaucracy of scribes and tax collectors over forty districts. It developed **hieroglyphics**, one of the oldest forms of writing, a **12-month calendar of 365 days**, the **Nilometer** and the water clock. Egyptians believed in life after death and **mummified** the wealthy; the pyramids show their architecture, the greatest being at Gizeh. The first foreign invasion came in 1670 BC, when the **Hyksos** invaded with bronze weapons.

## Kush and Meroe

**Kush** (present Sudan) also lived by the Nile. In **700 BC the Kushite king Kashta** rebelled and invaded Egypt; Kushite kings founded Egypt's **25th dynasty**. After an Egyptian expedition led by **Amasis and Potasimto captured Napata in 591 BC**, the capital moved to **Meroe**. Meroe had rainfall, cattle and crops, lay on trade routes to the Red Sea, and was a centre of **iron mining and smelting**, which gave Kush military power. It exported ivory, gold, ebony and ostrich feathers. Meroe declined after 300 AD and fell to an **Axumite army about 350 AD**.

## Axum

**Axum** (present Ethiopia) was the **third** ancient African centre of civilisation. It farmed with ox-drawn ploughs, domesticated elephants, and was the main commercial centre between the Mediterranean and the Indian Ocean, through its port **Adulis**. It used Ge'ez, Greek and Sabaean, and was perhaps the **first African state to mint its own coins**; the first king to circulate coins was **Edybis**. Under **King Ezana** in the early 4th century AD it conquered Meroe and became a **Christian kingdom**; by the 3rd century it ranked third among world powers after Rome and Persia.

## Rome in Africa

By legend **Rome** was founded around **753 BC** on the **River Tiber** by **Romulus**, who killed his twin Remus. North Africa, home of the **Berbers**, held **Phoenician** trading posts; the Phoenicians were the seamen of **Tyre and Sidon**, and their greatest settlement, **Carthage** (near modern Tunis), was founded about 814 BC. Rome fought Carthage in the **Punic Wars**; the **Second Punic War began in March 218 BC** (218–202 BC), and Carthage was destroyed after the **third, in 146 BC**. Roman civilisation in Africa was **mainly for economic reasons**. According to Ilevbare, North Africans fell into three groups: the subdued within the Roman province, the unsubdued within the frontier, and those outside it.
