# African Traditional Religion, Christianity and Islam

## Religion and culture

Kenny defines religion as any system that relates man to ultimate value; central to every religion is belief in God or something supernatural. **Religion is a product of culture**, part of a people's way of life, and is shaped by environment: riverine peoples worship water divinities and hill peoples worship hill divinities. The **practical encounter a person has with God** is **religious experience**, which precedes conviction and belief.

## African Traditional Religion

**African Traditional Religion** (the name that has gained most currency) is rooted in African culture. It has **no historical founder**, being as old as humanity, and **no scriptures**; its teachings are found in **oral traditions**: wise sayings, the Ifá corpus, fables, folktales, proverbs, songs, myths and taboos. It has **five fundamental beliefs**:

1. **God**, the self-existing Supreme Being and Creator, with many names: Olodumare (Yoruba), Chineke (Igbo), **Osanobua** (Edo), **Soko** (Nupe).
2. **Divinities**, intermediaries through whom God is reached.
3. **Ancestors**, the "living dead" (Mbiti), who protect and guide the living.
4. **Spirits**, which inhabit natural objects.
5. **Magic and medicine**: manipulating unseen forces, and using plants to heal.

These beliefs are interrelated, and the religion is rich in ethical values such as honesty and readiness to help the needy.

## Christianity

Christianity came from Asia. Mark is held to have brought it to **Alexandria** about 42–49 CE, and the Ethiopian eunuch baptised by Philip is often cited. The **four early centres north of the Sahara** were **Egypt**, **Roman Africa** (Tunisia, Libya, Algeria and Morocco, the second centre), **Abyssinia (Ethiopia)** and **Nubia (Sudan)**. African fathers such as Athanasius, Origen, Tertullian and Augustine shaped the faith. South of the Sahara, the church came with the Portuguese in the 15th century; 19th-century missions pursued the **"three Cs"**: Christianity, Civilisation and Commerce. The training of **black missionaries** such as **Ajayi Crowther** helped it spread.

## Islam

About 500 million Muslims live in Africa, more than a quarter of the world's Muslims. Africa gave Islam its **first mu'adhin, Bilal ibn Rabah**. Muslims took **Egypt and Libya from the Byzantine Empire in 642 AD**, Islam reached East Africa early through Indian Ocean trade, and it spread south along **five broad trans-Saharan trade routes**.

## Interaction

European Christian culture met African culture most clearly in **marriage**, with "one man, one wife" imposed on converts, and in burial rites. Islam interacted with African culture through **chieftaincy titles** among others.
