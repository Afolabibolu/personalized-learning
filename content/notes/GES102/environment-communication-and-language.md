# Environment, Communication and Language

## Environment and culture

**Environment** is the surroundings or circumstances in which a person or animal lives: the factors around an organism that influence its behaviour, reproduction and survival, made up of living (biotic) and non-living parts. **Culture** has been defined as the **total way of life of a given society or people**. **Man** is the link between culture and environment.

The environment influences people's way of life, **clothing, food, types of house, jobs, economic activities, tools, beliefs and arts**. Climate decides crops: groundnuts and cotton in the north of Nigeria, cocoa, oil palm and rubber in the south. Topography shapes settlement: riverine peoples build houses on stilts and fish, while clay or grass decides building materials elsewhere. The Igbo's land problems, from leaching, shaped their way of life. **Technological change** transforms the environment, and reckless exploitation of natural resources leads to pollution, erosion and deforestation.

## Communication

Communication is "the art of expressing ideas and the science of transmitting information" (Williams). Its fundamental characteristics are that it is the **exchange of meaningful symbols**; a **dynamic and continuous process**, cyclical as feedback turns the receiver into a sender; **ubiquitous**, existing everywhere; and **irreversible**.

Its components are:

- **Stimulus**: the incentive, inspiration or feeling that encourages someone to speak, act or gesture.
- **Sender (encoder)**, who packages the message; the **message**; the **medium** (oral, written or non-verbal); the **channel**, which amplifies it (speech, writing, video, email, text); the **receiver (decoder)**; and **feedback**, the receiver's response.
- **Noise**: anything that stops the message reaching the receiver in its intended form. It may be **psychological** (tiredness, hunger, anger), **channel** (a faulty device, poor print) or **linguistic**, which includes semantic, phonological, grammatical ("Where did you bought it?") and morphological noise.

Communication is **verbal**, either **oral** (the primary form, with immediate feedback, but lacking permanence) or **written**, or **non-verbal**, including **kinesics** (facial expression, eye movement and other body movement), **haptics** (touch), proxemics (space) and **chronemics** (the use of time).

## Language

Language is the human system of communicating with words and symbols, and **linguistics** is its scientific study. In Africa, language carries culture: through it a people's proverbs, history and values pass from one generation to the next.
