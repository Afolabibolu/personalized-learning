# Complex Numbers

## Complex numbers

The number systems are ℕ ⊂ ℤ ⊂ ℚ ⊂ ℝ. x² + 1 = 0 has no real root, so the system is extended to the **complex numbers**: **z = x + iy**, with x and y real and **i² = −1**. x is the **real part** and y the **imaginary part**.

- **Addition**: (x₁ + iy₁) + (x₂ + iy₂) = (x₁ + x₂) + i(y₁ + y₂).
- **Multiplication**: (x₁x₂ − y₁y₂) + i(x₁y₂ + x₂y₁). (2 − 3i)(3 + 2i) = 12 − 5i.
- The **conjugate** of z = x + iy is **z̄ = x − iy**, and **z z̄ = x² + y² = |z|²**.
- **Division**: multiply the top and bottom by the conjugate of the bottom. (3 − 4i)/(5 + 2i) = 7/29 − (26/29)i.

x² + 6x + 18 = 0 has roots −3 ± 3i. If a polynomial has **real coefficients**, its **complex roots come in conjugate pairs**. Since 2 − 3i is a root of x⁴ − 4x³ + 14x² − 4x + 13, so is 2 + 3i. Then x² − 4x + 13 is a factor, the quotient is x² + 1, and the roots are **2 ± 3i and ±i**.

## The Argand diagram

z = x + iy is plotted as the point (x, y). The **modulus** is |z| = r = √(x² + y²), and the **argument** is θ = arg z, with tan θ = y/x. The **polar form** is **z = r(cos θ + i sin θ)**.

When complex numbers are **multiplied**, the moduli multiply and the arguments **add**. When they are **divided**, the moduli divide and the arguments **subtract**.

A **locus** condition such as |z + 3| = 2|z − 2| becomes an equation in x and y. That one gives (x − 11/3)² + y² = (10/3)², a **circle** with centre (11/3, 0) and radius 10/3.

## De Moivre's theorem

**(cos θ + i sin θ)ⁿ = cos nθ + i sin nθ**, for every integer n.

- Comparing real and imaginary parts gives multiple-angle formulas: **cos 4β = 8cos⁴β − 8cos²β + 1**.
- If z = cos θ + i sin θ, then **zᵐ + 1/zᵐ = 2 cos mθ**.
- Powers: 1 + i = √2(cos π/4 + i sin π/4), so **(1 + i)¹⁰ = 32i**.

## Roots

The equation zⁿ = r(cos θ + i sin θ) has **n distinct roots**:

**z = r^(1/n)[cos((θ + 2kπ)/n) + i sin((θ + 2kπ)/n)]**, k = 0, 1, ..., n − 1.

The cube roots of 1 are **1, −½ ± (√3/2)i**, and the cube roots of −8 are 1 + i√3, −2 and 1 − i√3.
